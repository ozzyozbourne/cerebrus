Systems: CS-3, MemoryX, SwarmX, CS-4

A wafer has 44 GB of memory. Frontier models need hundreds of gigabytes to terabytes. Cerebras uses two opposite strategies to cross that gap: for training it streams weights through the wafer; for inference it pins weights onto several wafers and streams tokens through them.

  • Explain how inference fits a 70B model onto wafers, and estimate how many wafers a model needs.

Training: weight streaming

Early CS-1/CS-2 work mapped a whole model onto the wafer, layer by layer, in space ("layer-pipelined"). That caps model size at wafer SRAM. From 2021, Cerebras moved training to weight streaming: activations stay on the wafer, weights live off-wafer in MemoryX (DRAM plus flash, which also performs the weight update), and the whole wafer computes one layer at a time. SwarmX "handles the broadcast of weights from MemoryX to the entire cluster and effectively reduces (sums) gradients in the opposite direction" Vendor.

Inference: pin the weights, stream the tokens

Inference flips it. Decode is bandwidth-bound (Chapter 1), so the winning move is to keep every weight in SRAM and never fetch it from anywhere: "we don't need to access external memory to load parameters because they're already there" Vendor. The cost is capacity. Llama 3.1-70B needs about 140 GB at 16-bit, so at launch in August 2024 its 80 layers were spread across four CS-3 systems connected over Ethernet, each holding a contiguous block of layers, with tokens flowing wafer to wafer like a pipeline.

How many wafers does a model need?

Weights only, pinned in SRAM (44 GB per WSE-3), with a slice reserved for KV cache and activations. Compare with GPUs' HBM capacity.

Check yourself

Why does weight streaming suit training but not low-latency inference?

Training processes a large batch per weight load, so streaming each layer's weights once per step is amortised. Decode uses each weight for one token per sequence; streaming weights would make it as bandwidth-bound as a GPU.

What does SwarmX do on the backward pass?

Sums (reduces) gradients from all systems on the way back to MemoryX, which applies the update.

Roughly how many WSE-3 wafers to hold a 405B model at 16-bit?

810 GB of weights ÷ 44 GB ≈ 19 wafers before any KV cache — or about 5 at 4-bit.
