Appendix B · Reference

CUDA to CSL, budgets and constants

Three reference cards for writing your own kernel: a map from CUDA concepts to their CSL and wafer equivalents (and to TT-Metalium), a budget card for one PE on WSE-3 and WSE-2 with a checker for your design, and the cost constants this course measured in the SDK simulator.

Use this appendix to

  • Translate a CUDA habit (a launch, a barrier, an atomic, occupancy) into what you actually do on the wafer, or learn that there is no equivalent and why.
  • Check a kernel design against every per-PE limit before you write it: memory, colors, task IDs, queues, microthreads and routes.
  • Estimate a kernel's cycle count from measured constants, and know which chapter measured each one.

CUDA → CSL → TT-Metalium

The short version: CUDA launches many identical threads over one shared memory and lets hardware schedule and cache. CSL places a resident program on every PE, gives each one private memory, and moves data only along routes you declared. TT-Metalium sits between: per-core programs and explicit transfers, but large local memory and DRAM behind every core (Chapter 18). Rows marked no equivalent are where CUDA intuition misleads.

Putting the constants to work

A first estimate for a kernel on one PE is the sum of its vector ops (start-up + elements ÷ rate), plus ≈ 15 cycles per async transfer with its completion task, plus ≈ 9 cycles (WSE-3) per PE a value must pass through on the way. Lab 3 is exactly that sum: 90 + 2.5·n. Then measure on the TSC: the bank-clash and operand rules mean the first estimate can be off by 2×.
