## Findings

**A1 · high · "Experiment" paragraph + "A decode latency budget" + quiz Q5 + GEMV-rate select.** The page says WSE-3 f16 streaming "match[es] its ~4 f16 multiply-accumulates per cycle (note 22)". That is a simulator figure, and the vendor contradicts it. HC2024 (hc24.txt l.407–421) says the WSE-3 core is "8-way SIMD for 16b data (FP/BF16)" with "Full bandwidth for full SIMD performance". Ch 3 (l.214) teaches the same 8-way figure [Vendor, HC2024]. So the measured 7.5 B/cycle is about half the vendor's peak f16 rate. At 8/cycle, GEMV is ~2,600 cycles per layer, about half the 5,060-cycle budget rather than "the whole per-layer budget". That changes the headline conclusion ("streaming a region's own weights … is the biggest single term, about the size of the whole per-layer budget") and the "~2,000 tok/s at the kernel rate" one-user bound (×2 → ~4,000). **Fix:** in the experiment paragraph, say: "The simulator runs f16 @fmach at ~4/cycle on WSE-3, half the 8-way SIMD that HC2024 states [Vendor]. Whether hardware or production kernels reach 8/cycle is not public." Add a "15 B/cycle · 8-way SIMD peak (HC2024)" option to `#lb-rate`. Soften the conclusion to "between half and all of the per-layer budget", and qualify Q5's answer and the ~2,000 figure in the same way. The same correction applies to Ch 1, Ch 18 and note 25, which quote "~2,000 at the measured SDK rate".

**A2 · high · "The speed history, explained", closing paragraph.** It says: "Clock and kernel streaming rate, the hardware-bound terms, did not change." The table row above it quotes the Oct 2024 blog: "re-written or optimized … MatMul" kernels. The page's own budget paragraph also lists "production kernels that beat this naive one" as a lever. Streaming rate is a property of the kernel, so MatMul is the most likely lever of all. **Fix:** "The clock did not change. The MatMul kernel, which sets the streaming rate, was rewritten, so the plausible levers are kernel rate, collectives, overlap and tokens per pass."

**A3 · med · Speed-history row "Nov 2024 · Llama 3.1-405B".** The row says: "The long prompt halves the speed: attention over a 100K-token KV cache, the late-arriving term in the budget." The page's own budget model contradicts this. For 405B, a layer holds 810 GB ÷ 126 ≈ 6.4 GB of weights. The KV read for 100K context is 100,000 × 4,096 B ≈ 0.41 GB per layer, which adds ~6% to the GEMV, not 100%. (Even for 70B at 128K the widget adds only ~1,600 cycles to 5,190.) The cause of the halving is not published. Possible causes include more wafers or longer pipelines to hold 52 GB of KV, prefill interference, and attention kernels slower than streaming. The causal claim is also covered only by the row's [Vendor] tag. **Fix:** "The long prompt halves the speed. Cerebras does not say why. Under this chapter's budget, reading the KV adds only ~6% per layer, so attention kernels slower than weight streaming, or extra wafers for KV, must account for most of it [Derived/Unverified]."

**A4 · med · "Two ceilings" callout + `#cap` widget + `#lb-rate` option "24 B/cycle · HC22 port peak".** The headline 21 PB/s (and WSE-2's 20 PB/s) is the sum of all SRAM ports, reads and writes. HC22 gives the WSE-2 datapath as "2 full reads + 1 full write per cycle" of 64 b = 24 B/cycle, and 24 B × 1.1 GHz × 850k PEs ≈ 22 PB/s. A GEMV cannot use all of that for weights, because the write port and at least part of the read bandwidth carry the accumulator. So "~7,500 tok/s at the headline 21 PB/s" overstates the weight-streaming bound, and "24 B/cycle" is not a weight-streaming rate. **Fix:** add one sentence to the callout: "The headline counts every read and write port; a GEMV streams weights through at most the read ports, so its true ceiling is a fraction of this." Relabel the option "24 B/cycle · all SRAM ports, WSE-2 (HC22) — upper bound, not reachable for weights", or replace it with A1's 15 B/cycle option.

**A5 · med · "Two ceilings", last sentence of the third paragraph.** "Earlier chapters' '~18,000 tok/s from the GEMV benchmark' is the four-wafer figure …" No chapter now contains 18,000. Ch 1 and Ch 18 were already changed to the 7,500 / 600,000 / ~2,000 framing (grep: "18,000" appears only in Ch 9). The arithmetic does not reproduce it either: the f32 rate gives ~86,000 aggregate and ~1,070 per user. **Fix:** delete the sentence, or say "Earlier drafts of this course quoted …".

**A6 · low · "The model" → Collectives.** "~8 collectives, each spanning ~212 hops." A broadcast or chain-reduce across a 212 × 212 region covers ~212 hops per dimension, ~424 from corner to corner (or ~212 only with row and column phases overlapped or a centre root). The slider maximum of 424 suggests the builder knew this. **Fix:** add "(one dimension; ~424 corner to corner)" so that what-if A is read as optimistic, not pessimistic.

**A7 · low · KV-cache arithmetic and "Weights per PE".** "~36 GB left … 13 users at 8K" ignores the fixed per-PE code and task overhead, which is ~4.6 KB on WSE-3 for a minimal CSL program (note 13). Across 3.6 M PEs that is ~16 GB, leaving ~20 GB (~7 users). The hedge "none held activations or code" is present, but the quiz (Q3, "About 13 users at 8K fit") drops it. "81% of each 48 KB" is 79% with 48 KiB. **Fix:** in Q3 say "at most ~13 (fewer once code and buffers take their share; a minimal CSL program already needs ~4.6 KB/PE, Ch 10)".

**A8 · low · "Speculative decoding on an SRAM machine", second paragraph.** "Each f16 multiply-accumulate reads a weight and an accumulator and writes the accumulator, so a k-wide pass … costs about k times." HC22 says accumulators live in the local cache ("Used for high frequency access data such as accumulators"), so the SRAM-port argument is not the binding one. The conclusion still holds for a different reason: a GEMV that already runs at full SIMD width is compute-bound per MAC, and k-wide work needs k× the MACs. **Fix:** "If the GEMV already runs the datapath at full SIMD width, a k-wide pass needs k× the multiply-accumulates, so it costs ~k×." Also say that the 5,648-cycle k = 1 kernel differs from the 5,228-cycle table kernel (it has an extra loop structure), to avoid confusion.

**A9 · low · "Inter-wafer budget".** "The four hops are three wafer-to-wafer hops plus the return trip" is an interpretation. HC2024 says only "4 hops required". **Fix:** add [Derived] or "presumably".

**A10 · low · "What sparsity does in practice".** It calls 16-bit value + 16-bit index "the MemoryX working-copy format of Chapter 8". Ch 8 presents it as the 32-bit fabric packet format for streamed weights ("weight (16b) + index (16b)"), not a storage format. **Fix:** "(the streamed-weight packet format of Chapter 8)". The 50% break-even arithmetic is correct.

**A11 · low · Provenance tags.** (a) "approximately 20 CS-3 systems" for Kimi is tagged "Vendor, via press", but build-ch09.md says it is press-only. Use `indep` "Press" plus `unv`. (b) LPX rack "128 or 256 LPUs [Unverified]" disagrees with Ch 17, which says most reports and NVIDIA's localised pages give 256 and that 128 looks like an error. Align with Ch 17. (c) The CS-4 row's "twice the power for higher operating frequencies" is verbatim vendor text (cs4.txt), so the [Unverified] tag next to it should cover only the per-user tok/s claim, or be removed.

**A12 · low · "Pipeline decode" paragraph.** "At 2,100 tok/s, one token's full trip through all four wafers takes about 476 µs." Since Oct 2024, 2,100 tok/s includes speculative decoding, so one pass (trip) is ~476 µs × E. The page's own budget widget uses this (it prints "476 × E µs per pass"). **Fix:** "about 476 µs per output token; with speculation one trip may take longer and yield more than one token".

**A13 · low · "Context length shows up late".** At 128K, "~1,500 cycles per layer" is 1,590 by the page's own formula, so write "~1,600". It is "comparable to the collectives" only under what-if B (3,392). Under the default/A settings, the collectives are 15,600. Say "about half of router-speed collectives".
