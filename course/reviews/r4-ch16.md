## Errors

**E1 · med · #scale-out, last paragraph.** The page says: "These links are about 7× slower than one on-chip NoC link and about 10× slower than a chip's DRAM". The 7× mixes two chips: Blackhole's NoC link (86 GB/s) divided by Wormhole's Ethernet link (12.5 GB/s) gives 6.9. Taken chip by chip, the ratios are:
- Blackhole: 86 / 50 ≈ 1.7× (NoC) and 512 / 50 ≈ 10× (DRAM).
- Wormhole: 32 / 12.5 ≈ 2.6× (NoC) and 288 / 12.5 ≈ 23× (DRAM).

The much larger gap is in latency: 515–711 ns per Ethernet hop against 9 NoC cycles ≈ 7 ns, so about 70–100×. *Fix:* give both ratios for each chip and add the latency ratio. That ratio is the number that makes "how you split a model matters".

**E2 · med · #product-line peak bullets and #matmul closing paragraph.** The page says "581 TF on a 13 × 10 grid … 81% of the 130-core peak" and also "Peak utilisation was 93% on Wormhole and 96% on Blackhole". A reader sees about 580 TF described both as 81% and as 96%. The GEMM_FLOPS report defines utilisation as ideal cycles / actual cycles at the run's own fidelity. Its peaks (table maximum ≈ 94.9% device-based) are HiFi4 runs at about 160 TF, not 96% of 719/774 TF. The report's current tables also go higher than 581: BFP4/LoFi at 5120×6656×6656 reaches **641 TF, about 89%** of 130 × 4096 × 1.35 GHz. *Fix:* one sentence saying "utilisation is measured against the peak of the chosen fidelity", and update the LoFi best to about 640 TF (89%) [vendor-measured], or keep "~580" and label it as the prose figure.

**E3 · low · #noc-grid note / #product-line.** "The tt-metal GEMM benchmark ran on a 13 × 10 compute grid" uses 130 cores, but the same page says firmware now enables only 120. *Fix:* add "(run before the 2026 core cut)".

**E4 · low · #70b table.** The row "72.5 t/s/u … 2,269 tok/s total at batch 32" is not self-consistent: 72.5 × 32 = 2,320. The README defines T/S = T/S/U × batch, but its own row gives 2268.8, which implies 70.9. *Fix:* a footnote: "vendor table; 2,269 implies ~70.9 t/s/u". Ch 1's table also gives this ceiling as ≈130 (from ~70 GB), while Ch 16 and Ch 18 give 124 (74 GB in BFP8). Align Ch 1 to 74 GB → 124.

**E5 · low · #multicast, last paragraph.** "a wavelet's color decides its route at compile time" overstates the case. The course itself shows routes being switched at runtime (switch positions and control wavelets in Ch 4, and Lab 5's allreduce "rewriting its routes between stages", App A g-switch-route-positions). *Fix:* "routes are configured at compile time (and can be switched by control wavelets)".

**E6 · low · #matmul widget.** Ideal cycles divide by `gx*gy` even when fewer cores have work. For example, M = 256 on 13 × 10 uses only 8 rows, so 104 of 130 cores. The "TFLOPS if 100% utilised" readout is then overstated by up to 25%. *Fix:* divide by `used_x*used_y` and say so.

## Connecting gaps

**G1 · high · whole page: no links at all.** `grep -c "<a "` returns 0. The page cites Chapter 1, Chapters 4 and 5, Ch. 8, "Labs 2 and 3", "Chapters 4–13" and the glossary, all as plain text. A learner cannot jump from "SUMMA-like multicast matmul" to Lab 4, or from "decode ceiling" to Ch 1 or Ch 9. *Bridge:* link every chapter reference, link App A terms on first use, and add a "See also" line in #matmul → 14-lab-gemm.html and in #70b → 15-lab-cg.html#allreduce and 09-model-to-tokens.html. Effort S.

**G2 · high · Tensix vs PE: memory grain → compute grain.** #vs-pe and its table compare memory (1,536 KiB vs 48 KB) and the data unit, but never compute per cycle. The learner cannot answer "how many PEs is one Tensix?" *Bridge:* add a table row "Peak per unit per cycle":
- Tensix: 4,096 FLOP (LoFi) [ISA/GEMM_FLOPS].
- PE: 16 FLOP (8-way FP16 FMA, Ch 3) [vendor], or about 8 measured (≈4 f16 MAC/cycle, note 27).

So one Tensix ≈ 256 PEs (≈512 at measured rates) in compute but only ≈32 PEs in memory. That one ratio *is* the "grain" argument. Effort S.

**G3 · high · "decode is capped by DRAM bandwidth" → "what caps Cerebras then?"** The #70b ceiling and the quiz answer ("Cerebras inference instead keeps the weights in SRAM, but … four CS-3") imply that SRAM removes the limit. The course's own position (notes 6, 25–27, Ch 1 table) is different: one user on 4 × CS-3 is bounded at ~2,000–4,000 tok/s by per-region reads plus a latency chain of 80 layers, against ~2,100–2,314 measured. *Bridge:* add a two-row worked comparison after the Galaxy back-of-envelope: Wormhole Galaxy ceiling 124 → 72.5 measured (bandwidth-bound) vs 4 × CS-3 bound ~2,000–4,000 → ~2,100–2,314 (latency/communication-bound). Link Ch 1's bound table and Ch 9 "From model to tokens". Effort S.

**G4 · med · why Tenstorrent's granule is a 2 KiB tile (packet cost → tile size).** The page gives the hop cost (5 + 9h + 5 + flits) and Cerebras's 32-bit wavelets, but never connects them. *Bridge:* add a worked number. One 4-byte value sent 5 hops on Blackhole ≈ 5 + 45 + 5 + 2 = 57 cycles for 4 B. A 2 KiB tile is 5 + 45 + 5 + 33 = 88 cycles, so 23 B/cycle. On the wafer the same 4 B costs 2 cycles/hop with no header (note 20). Addressed packets have to amortise a header and the NIU latency, which a static color route does not. This explains why Tenstorrent computes on tiles and Cerebras on wavelets. Effort S.

**G5 · med · circular buffers ↔ CSL FIFOs / input queues.** The CSL↔Metalium table maps tasks, DSDs and multicast, but not the CB itself. That is the concept a Cerebras learner most needs. *Bridge:* add a row: CB with `cb_reserve_back`/`cb_push_back` ↔ CSL `@allocate_fifo` / fabric input queue back-pressure (Ch 5–6, App A g-fifo, g-back-pressure). Also note that Cerebras gets its "double buffering" from hardware queues and the router's back-pressure rather than from explicit slot counts. Effort S.

**G6 · med · Galaxy all-reduce ↔ Lab 5 allreduce.** #70b derives a tensor-parallel all-reduce cost over Ethernet (~µs, ~0.7 µs per hop). Lab 5 measured the on-wafer allreduce at ~958 + 100·W + 88·H cycles. Neither page puts them side by side, even though this is the clearest illustration of "one wafer = no chip boundary". *Bridge:* add one sentence plus numbers. Take a 32-participant reduce on the wafer at ~2 cycles/hop (≈ ns) against 515–711 ns/hop between Galaxy chips, and link Lab 5. Effort S.

**G7 · low · Tensix stepper → CB widget.** The stepper ends with "in steady state all five cores are busy at once", but the CB widget only shows reader, compute and writer. It never shows the inner unpack/math/pack overlap that justifies three TRISCs. *Bridge:* add a sentence in the CB caption: "compute's bar hides a three-stage pipeline (TRISC0/1/2) that overlaps the same way, using Dst half-sync instead of a CB". Effort S.
