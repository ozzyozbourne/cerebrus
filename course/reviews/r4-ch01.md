## Errors

**E1 · high · "Bound versus reality", paragraph after the table.** Quoted: "Compute is not the limit either: 140 GFLOP per token against 4 × 15.6 PF allows about 450,000 tok/s." This uses the aggregate figure that the same paragraph has just rejected for bandwidth. The per-region argument applies to compute too. One user's token is in 1/80 of the PEs, so it sees 62.4 PF ÷ 80 ≈ 780 TF, which caps one user at ≈ 5,600 tok/s. At the measured f16 kernel rate (~4 MAC/cycle per PE, notes 14/22/27) the cap is ≈ 2,000 tok/s. On the wafer, compute is in fact the *tighter* limit. Decode intensity (≈ 1 FLOP/B) is above the WSE-3 ridge (0.74), and the page's own roofline widget shows WSE-3 decode as COMPUTE-bound at 100% of peak. Ch09's "7.5 B/cycle" GEMV rate is itself a multiply-accumulate limit (ch09 line 356: "this AXPY kernel's limit (~4 f16 multiply-accumulates per cycle)"). So "per-region SRAM reads" in the bolded conclusion is better put as "per-region multiply-accumulate rate (≈ the SRAM read rate at 1 FLOP/B), plus latency and communication". Fix: replace the 450,000 sentence with the per-region compute cap and say that at intensity ≈ 1 the wafer's read and math limits coincide.

**E2 · high · "Decode speed bounds" calculator note (JS line 477).** This is still open from r3 G1, but it is worse than r3 described. For CS-3, 70B FP16, batch 1, the readout prints "intensity ≈ 1 vs ridge 0.743 → compute-bound", the *math* bar is marked "← binds", and the result is ≤ ~446,000 tok/s per user. The note then says "On the wafer this is a memory bound only … divide the bound by the chip count", which gives ~111,000. That contradicts the readout above it and the table below it (2,000–4,000). Fix: for CS-3, compute per-user = aggregate ÷ L (number of layers) for both memory and math. Print the aggregate across all users on a separate line. Drop the "memory bound only" and "divide by chip count" wording.

**E3 · med · "What this course covers".** Quoted: "Part III programs it: the CSL toolchain, then three labs with five original programs." This is stale. The course now has five labs (Ch 11–15) and seven programs (programs/01–07). The paragraph also skips Chapter 9 (From model to tokens), the chapter the decode section relies on ("Chapters 4, 5 and 9 show what that time is made of"). Fix: list Ch 9, and change to "five labs (Ch 11–15) built on seven programs".

**E4 · med · "Capacity cuts both ways".** Quoted: "four wafers give 176 GB, leaving about 36 GB for the KV cache and activations." This ignores the per-PE program/runtime overhead: ≥ 4,240 B of each 48 KB PE (note 13), ≈ 3.8 GB per wafer, ≈ 15 GB over four wafers. Real headroom is ≈ 20 GB, before any activations or buffers. Note 28 makes the same correction ("~13 users … ~7 after ~4.2 KB/PE code"). Fix: say "≈ 36 GB on paper, ≈ 20 GB after each PE's ~4 KB of code (Ch 3)".

**E5 · low · Bound table, Wormhole Galaxy row.** Quoted: "≈ 130" tok/s. Ch16 (lines 405/465) gives the same ceiling as ≈ 124 tok/s. It counts BlockFP8's shared exponents (≈ 74 GB, giving 8.1 ms per pass). Use 124 to match ch16. The "within about 2×" wording stays true (72.5 is 58%).

**E6 · low · Capacity map, H100 RF+smem bar.** The bar spans 20–400 TB/s (`rf.lo = 2e13`). 21 PB/s ÷ 20 TB/s ≈ 1,050×, but the text and the arrow label say "50–700×", which needs a low end of 30 TB/s. Set lo = 3e13 so the bar matches the text.

**E7 · low · Batching derivation.** Quoted: "That is why sources quote 'about 50–100 FLOP/byte' for batch 64 on a 70B model <span Derived>." A quotation from sources should not carry a Derived tag. Tag it with the source (research notes) or Unverified. Also, the "~66 MB" (132 × 512 KB) used in the RF+smem total and the 670× ratio is really 67.6 MB (69 MB in KiB). The effect is negligible, but the page should say "≈ 67 MB".

**E8 · low · Calculator, B200.** The calculator always uses B200's 9 PF FP4 peak, even for FP16/FP8 weights. The roofline widget warns about this; the calculator does not. Add the same note, or use 2.25 PF BF16 / 4.5 PF FP8 (ch17 table).

## Explanation problems

**X1 · "Measured: one PE's roofline" vs the WSE-3 ridge table.** The callout measures matvec at 0.17 FLOP/B and says "Memory is not the limit; the arithmetic is". Two sections earlier the learner was told the WSE-3 ridge is 0.6–0.75. On the page's own numbers, 0.17 < 0.74 should mean bandwidth-bound, so the callout reads as a contradiction. The missing step is that the ridge depends on the format. At fp32, ~1 MAC/cycle = 2 FLOP/cycle against 32 B/cycle gives a ridge of ≈ 0.06, so 0.17 sits above it. Fix: add one sentence plus a row "fp32 ridge ≈ 2 ÷ 32 ≈ 0.06 (measured peak)" to the one-PE table.

**X2 · "Why batching helps".** "Batching helps until the KV cache is as large as the weights" is stated without numbers. Give the asymptote. As B → ∞, intensity → 2P ÷ (context × KV bytes per token) = 140 GFLOP ÷ 0.65 GB ≈ 213 FLOP/B at 2k context and ≈ 53 at 8k. So for a 70B model at 2k context, **no batch size** reaches the H100's HBM ridge of 295. This is a sharper takeaway than the current one, and it fits in one line.

**X3 · Rule "FLOPs per token ≈ 2 × parameters".** The page uses 140 GFLOP/token and "64 × 140 GFLOP" without stating the rule. Add it to the worked example in #decode.

**X4 · KV formula.** "8 KV heads of width 128" assumes the learner knows grouped-query attention. Add a half-sentence: "Llama-3 shares 8 K/V heads among its 64 query heads (grouped-query attention), which shrinks the cache 8×."

**X5 · Cross-references are plain text.** The page has zero `<a>` links. Every "Chapter N" and the "Terms used here" box should link: to the chapter, and to Appendix A for ridge point, intensity, KV cache, PE and fabric. Ch09 does this (16 links). The "Where the wafer's headline numbers come from" and "What this course covers" h2s also have no id, so they cannot be linked to.

**X6 · Quiz Q6.** The answer names two assumptions for "WSE-3 decode at 100% of peak". It should name a third: that the analyst 15.6 PF dense peak is reachable. Measured f16 kernels get about half the 8-wide SIMD rate (notes 14/22/27, ch09).

## Connecting gaps

**G1 · high · "make on-chip SRAM big enough" (three answers) → "Chapter 2 shows why a whole wafer can be one chip".** No page in the course says *why* 44 GB of SRAM needs a wafer, i.e. why a GPU cannot simply add SRAM. The missing link is SRAM area. A 5-nm-class bitcell is ~0.02 µm² [Indep, check], and 44 GB = 3.5 × 10¹¹ bits, so the bitcells alone take ≈ 7,000 mm². With periphery that is roughly 15,000 mm²: a third of the wafer, and ~18 H100-sized reticle dies. Bridge: a short paragraph plus one bar ("44 GB SRAM area vs one reticle, 858 mm²") at the end of #three-answers, handing off to ch02 #reticle. Effort S–M.

**G2 · high · "layer-pipelined, each layer read only by its own region" → the single-user bound.** "Region", "layer-pipelined", "tensor-parallel" and "pipeline split" carry the chapter's main conclusion, but none is defined here; ch08/ch09 define them later. Bridge: a small figure of 4 wafers sliced into 80 layer bands. One user's token sits in one band; other users fill the other bands. Caption: "one user sees 1/80 of the bandwidth *and* of the FLOPs (E1); the total is the sum over users". Also add a forward link to ch09 #region mapping. Effort M.

**G3 · med · roofline bound → "latency and communication" conclusion.** The page says decode is set by something "the roofline does not model", but never supplies the per-token time budget that would make this concrete. Bridge: 2,314 tok/s ≈ 430 µs per token ÷ 80 layers ≈ 5.4 µs ≈ 4,600 cycles per layer at 850 MHz. Of that, the f16 GEMV on 38.9 KB per PE takes ~5,200 cycles at the measured rate or ~2,600 at 8-wide (ch09 Q). The rest is reduction, hops and 3 wafer crossings. One worked line, with a link to ch09. Effort S.

**G4 · med · analyst peak on the roofline → what a PE actually achieves.** The WSE-3 flat roof is the 15.6 PF analyst figure, and the one-PE table uses the vendor 8-wide SIMD. The measured callout is fp32 only. The measured f16 rate (~4 MAC/cycle on WSE-3, notes 14/22) is never connected to the roof. Bridge: add a dashed "measured f16 kernel ≈ 6 PF" roof to the WSE-3 SRAM level, plus a row in the one-PE table. This is the ch01 side of r3 G4 for ch02. Effort S.

**G5 · low · Little's law → why a PE needs no threads.** "A PE needs only a few bytes in flight" is asserted. Bridge: 23 GB/s × 1 cycle (~1.2 ns) ≈ 27 B, i.e. about 7 f32 words, against 2 MB on the H100. One line of arithmetic. Effort S.
