## Errors

**E1 (med). Quiz Q7 gives the wrong per-instruction overhead and contradicts Q5 and the callout.**
Location: Check yourself, Q7.
Quoted text: "kernel 0 is already cheaper on WSE-2 (96,630 vs 103,724 cycles per step at T = 32, about 27 vs 30 cycles of per-instruction overhead)".
What is right: at T = 32 there are 1,024 instructions per step. WSE-2: 96,630 / 1,024 = 94.4 cycles, minus 64 for the clash, leaves **≈30**. WSE-3: 101.3 − 64 = **≈37**.
- The page's own Q5 says "about 37 cycles per instruction". The callout says "≈ 37–38 at 16–32".
- "27 vs 30" are the intercepts of the least-squares fits (27.2 / 29.6). Those fits have slopes 2.12 / 2.32, not 2, so the intercepts are not per-instruction overheads.
Fix: write "about 30 vs 37 cycles".

**E2 (low). The page says the kernel-1 fit is "only approximate" and gives the wrong reason.**
Location: callout "Where the other two-thirds go", kernel-1 paragraph, and §measure "Kernel 1".
Quoted text: "The fitted 1.87 is lower because the per-element cost falls as T grows (1.95 at T = 32): the fit averages over T = 8–32 and is only approximate".
What is right: the fit is per k, 1.87·Mt·Nt + 77, so the all-in cost per element is 1.87 + 77/(Mt·Nt).
- T = 16: 1.87 + 77/256 = 2.17, against 2.18 measured.
- T = 32: 1.87 + 77/1,024 = 1.95, against 1.95 measured.
- T = 8: the fit gives 1,574 against 1,594.
So the fit matches the data well. The slope is lower than the all-in cost only because the 77-cycle start-up per k is spread over more elements as T grows.
Fix: say that, and drop "only approximate".

## Connecting gaps

**G1 (high, effort S for the text, M to measure). The Chapter 6 fix for scalar-loop overhead and bank clashes is never linked.**
Gap: Ch 6 "@map + DSRs" → Ch 14 "kernel 0's fixed cost" and "remove the bank clash".
- Chapter 6 (`06-dsds.html#dsrs`) measured the cure for exactly kernel 0's overhead. In its matvec, a scalar loop of @fmacs plus @increment_dsd_offset costs **N + 35** per column, and `@map` over DSRs loaded with `.save_address = true` costs **N + 2**.
- The same section shows the SDK matvec shifting A by one word "to avoid bank conflicts". That is the exercise-4 fix.
- Ch 14 cites Ch 6 only for "33–35 cycles per step". Its list of levers (hoist, unroll, async) leaves out @map/DSRs, the one lever the course has already measured.
Bridge:
- In the callout, add: "Chapter 6's matvec removes almost all of this with @map over DSRs (N + 2 vs N + 35 per column), and avoids the clash by shifting one array by a word, the same fix as exercise 4."
- In "Kernel 0's fixed cost", add @map as lever 1. Sketch: for each k, load the A column into `src1` without save_address, load C into dest/src0 with `.save_address = true`, then `@map` over row k of B (stride Kt).
- Make it exercise 8's suggested lever.

**G2 (med, effort S). Kernel 1's 2-D zero-stride DSDs are used with no explanation.**
Gap: Ch 6 "stride 0 broadcasts one value" (1-D) → kernel 1's `|j, i|{Nt, Mt} -> A_tile[i]` and `-> B_tile[j * Kt]`.
Missing:
- that leaving an induction variable out of the index makes that dimension's stride 0;
- which operand repeats along which axis;
- why the increments are Mt (A) and 1 (B) per k;
- why two instructions and a T_tile are needed instead of one. The course only ever shows @fmacs with a scalar multiplier, so there is no single "C += vector × vector" op.
The only explanation is half a sentence ("2-D DSDs with a zero stride broadcast column k of A and row k of B").
Bridge: a 2 × 2 worked walk (Mt = Nt = 2) listing the addresses each DSD visits for (j, i) = (0,0), (0,1), (1,0), (1,1), plus one sentence on why @fmacs cannot take a vector multiplier. This would also explain the open question of why zero-stride @fmuls runs below 1 element per cycle: one operand rereads the same word.

**G3 (med, effort S). The page never computes the per-PE ridge point.**
Gap: Ch 1 roofline (intensity, ridge) → Ch 14 "compute-bound regime".
The page states the intensity ("2T² words for T³ multiply-adds"; "a square tile does T/2 multiply-adds per word received") and a receive ceiling of ~1 word/cycle. It never puts them together.
Bridge: "With ~1 word/cycle in and at best 1 MAC/cycle, the ridge is T/2 = 1, so T = 2. The measured crossover is at T ≈ 4 (2,310 vs 2,492) because the 520-cycle fixed cost per broadcast and the ~3.5 cycles per MAC both shift it." Then point back to `01-memory-wall.html`'s roofline. This makes "compute-bound ≠ at peak" a reading of the roofline rather than a slogan.
