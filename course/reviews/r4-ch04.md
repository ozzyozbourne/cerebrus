# R4 audit: Chapter 4, The fabric (04-fabric.html, ch04)

**Verdict: Mostly correct. Explanation quality: good.** The SDK-sourced core is accurate: register bits, control payload, switch trace, filters, the color counts in the labs, and the quiz arithmetic. The page has two stale or misleading numbers and several smaller slips. Its main weakness is the bridges. It never shows the CE-side code that puts a wavelet on a color, and it never links to anything: it has zero `href`s to other chapters or the glossary.

Experiment: I copied program 03 to `cerebras-sdk/tmp/r4-ch04/`, gave it one color with middle routes `rx {WEST, RAMP} → tx {RAMP, EAST}`, and ran it on `--arch=wse2` (P=4, M=8). It compiles. It then **hangs**: sim.log stops at cycle 1,706 and the host was still waiting after 90 s. I killed it and deleted the dir.

## Errors

**E1 · med · #latency, end of the method paragraph.** "A CE that adds as well as relays (Lab 2) costs about 7 cycles per PE [Measured, Ch. 12]". Ch 12 #hop-cost says 7.2/5.0 is an own-clock artifact. The true CE-forwarded hop is **≈9.2 cycles on WSE-3 and 6.0 on WSE-2**, and Ch 12's model uses c ≈ 9/6. CCN 24 and glossary "Hop" agree. Fix: "about 9 cycles per PE on WSE-3 and 6 on WSE-2 (Ch 12 #hop-cost)". *Still open (from r3-gaps-ch03-04 G1).*

**E2 · med · #color-budget planner items.** The planner lists "4-neighbour send WITHOUT switches (4)" and "4-neighbour send WITH switches (topic-06) (1)" next to "2-D 4-neighbour exchange (game of life) (8)". A learner reads these as three ways to do the same job and concludes that switches cut 8 colors to 1. They don't. topic-06 has **one** sender and four pure receivers. In an all-PE exchange, every PE must accept from RAMP and from a neighbour on the same color. That needs two rx directions, which is illegal on WSE-3 and is the "Broken" pattern on WSE-2. So the parity doubling (8) stays. Fix: relabel the items "one PE sends to its 4 neighbours: 4 colors / 1 with switches (topic-06)". Add a hint line: "switches save colors over time at one sender; they do not remove the checkerboard".

**E3 · low · #color-budget, paragraph under the planner.** "20 (used internally by the runtime for switching)". `memcpy/sys_params.csl` names color 20 `SYS_RXCTX`, "the routing from context buffer … persistent context switch". Ch 7 #reserves says "20 context switch". Coming right after the switches section, "switching" reads as fabric switches. Fix: "20 (context-switch routing, SYS_RXCTX)".

**E4 · low · #filters.** "This is the same index field that Chapter 5 uses for sparse data." Ch 5 has no sparse or index material. The index-plus-value wavelet is in Ch 3 #sparsity and Ch 6 #index-offset. Fix the reference.

**E5 · low · first figcaption.** "so the fabric is lossless and deadlock-free". Per-color buffers stop one color from blocking another color's buffers. They do not stop a program from deadlocking: a blocked data task or a full queue stalls the sender for good (Ch 5 #rules, `@block`; the hangs in CCN 7/11/21). Fix: "lossless, and a stalled color does not block other colors; a program can still deadlock itself (Ch 5)".

**E6 · low · #checkerboard.** "On WSE-2 it compiles and gives wrong answers." This is untagged, and my run did not give wrong answers: it hung. Fix: "On WSE-2 it compiles (Measured) and then hangs or gives wrong sums".

**E7 · low · #color-budget.** "(The library source names 4 routable system colors; the README's count of 6 is not broken down.)" The README's numbers add up: 21 + 6 + 4 = 31. That is the 5-bit activatable ID space ("`.activate` only accepts color 0~30", sys_params WARNING 1). So "6" counts memcpy's 4 colors plus IDs it uses as entrypoints, not routable colors. *Still open (from r3-gaps G6).*

## Explanation problems

**X1 · #checkerboard figcaption.** "WSE-2's color swap does the checkerboard in hardware … with one identical route on every PE." Read quickly, this sounds as if swap fixes the one-color reduce. It still uses two colors. What it removes is the per-parity *layout* code, and topic-14's route is a forward (`rx WEST → tx {RAMP, EAST}`), not an add-and-send. Add: "it still uses two colors; it saves writing parity-dependent routes".

**X2 · #router-numbers.** The row "fabric coordinate field 10 bits / 11 bits" comes right after "Why static? no addresses in wavelets", with no explanation. A learner can take it as a wavelet address field. It is the PE's own coordinate register, read with `tile_config.get_fabric_coord` (it is masked by `fabric_coord_mask`). Say so, and note that 11 bits allows a dimension above 1,023.

**X3 · #control-wavelets.** "a fabric output DSD with `.control = true`" and "@fmovs fabric-to-fabric relay" (#latency method) use fabric DSDs, which Ch 6 #fabric-dsds teaches. Add a one-line gloss: "a DSD that names a queue/color instead of memory; Ch 6".

**X4 · #latency calculator.** It models only ramps + hops + (length − 1). The measured round trip has a fixed ~36–44 cycles, from DSD start-up of ≈15–17 cycles per op (CCN 16/23), so short streams read ~40 cycles low against TSC. Add an "op start-up ≈ 17" term. *Still open (r3 G10).*

**X5 · #checkerboard.** "a 2-D pattern alternates by row as well". The quiz then expects 8 colors, but no figure shows the 2-D case. A 4×4 sketch with the E/W/N/S colors at two parities would make the 8 visible. *Still open (fixlog "left for later").*

**X6 · #teardown.** This is the only mechanism section with no figure or trace (pending bit → task 29 → rewrite → release). *Still open (r3 G13).*

## Connecting gaps

**G1 · high · "route in layout.csl" → "what the PE code does to send/receive on that color".** The page shows only `@set_color_config`. A route of `rx RAMP` says "accept from my own CE", but nothing shows how a CE puts a wavelet onto c0 or takes one off. That needs a `fabout_dsd`/`fabin_dsd` on a queue, plus `@initialize_queue(oq, .{.color = c})` on WSE-3 (or `.fabric_color` on WSE-2). The learner first meets this pairing in Ch 6 and Lab 2. Bridge: put the two halves of program 03 side by side. Left: the layout route. Right: its 4 lines in `pe_program.csl` (`out_dsd`, `in_dsd`, `@initialize_queue`, `@fadds(out_dsd, in_dsd, v_dsd, .async)`). Caption: "router half / CE half; Ch 6 #fabric-dsds explains the right side". Effort S.

**G2 · med · "one rx direction per color on WSE-3" → "so how do many-to-one patterns work?".** The page says merging "is impossible" and stops there. Bridge, one paragraph: many-to-one is done by (a) a chain through CEs (Lab 2; costs ~9 cycles per PE, see E1), (b) one color per source, or (c) switches or teardown that change rx over time (`<collectives_2d>` reduce). Point forward to Ch 12 and Ch 15. Effort S.

**G3 · med · "back-pressure" → "multicast".** A multicast can stall too: when one branch's buffer is full, can the wavelet still go to the other branches? The page doesn't say, and it matters for Lab 2's broadcast and memcpy's row fan. Bridge: state the rule if a source has it. If not, mark it unverified and suggest a small simulator test (block the data task on one PE of program 05, then time the others). Effort M.

**G4 · med · no navigation links.** Ch 4 mentions Chapters 3, 5, 7, 10, 12, 13 and 16 and a dozen glossary terms (wavelet, color, ramp, back-pressure, checkerboard, control wavelet, teardown, filter). It links none of them; the only link on the page is the top-bar Glossary button. Bridge: link each "Chapter N" mention to its section. Link first uses to `appendix-a-glossary.html#g-…` (ids exist: `g-color`, `g-back-pressure`, `g-checkerboard`, `g-control-wavelet`, …). Effort S.

**G5 · med · "pure hop 1–2 cycles" → "CE-forwarded hop ~9".** Even with E1 fixed, the page never says *why* a relaying PE costs 4–9× a router hop. Bridge, one worked sum: ramp down + queue + DSD op issue/start + ramp up ≈ 9 (WSE-3). Use it to motivate "keep traffic in the router; relay through CEs only when needed" (which #relay states without numbers). Effort S.

**G6 · low-med · hop measurement not reproducible.** The key Measured table (2.0/1.0 cycles per hop) has a one-line method only, and its program was deleted. *Still open (r3 G4).* Add a collapsible ~25-line program or a programs/08. Effort M.

**G7 · low-med · objective "use switches and filters" has no hands-on path.** *Still open (r3 G7).* Add a "Try it" box that runs topic-06 and topic-08 with `commands_wse3.sh` and compares the output with the trace table. Flag topic-14, histogram-torus and 25-pt-stencil as WSE-2-only in SDK 2.10.1. Effort S.

**G8 · low · Ch 3 → Ch 4 hop wording.** Ch 3 #wavelets says "hop one router per cycle" with no qualifier. Ch 4 then measures 2.0 on the WSE-3 simulator. A one-word "about … (simulator: 2 on WSE-3, Ch 4)" in Ch 3 removes the apparent contradiction. (Ch 3's fix; r3 G11.)

## Checked and fine
- Route register: TX 0x10|0x04 = 0x14; RX NORTH 0x100 (WSE-2) → 0x114; 0x60 (WSE-3) → 0x74. Matches `target/wse2.csl` and `wse3.csl` `fabric_io`.
- Control payload: opcodes 0–3, entrypoint at bit 16, cmd0 opcode at bit 22, CE-ignore at bit 24, null entrypoint 31. SWITCH_ADV + ignore → 0x015F0000 is correct (`control.csl`).
- Chip constants: NUM_COLORS 24; SWITCH_REGS_PER_COLOR 1/4; queues 8/6 and 8/8; filters 4+2 and 6; half-wavelet counters on WSE-3; coord masks 0x03ff/0x07ff.
- memcpy: colors 21/22/23/20, and the switching-capable list 0–9, 12, 13, 16, 17, 20, 21 (sys_params WARNING 4).
- Counter-filter example: matches the `memcpyh2d.csl` Example 2 table.
- topic-06: layout, send order and run.py asserts (E 0, S 2, N 4, W 6). The 8-row trace is correct. The topic-07 quote is verbatim.
- topic-08: routes, `min_idx`/`max_idx`, and the 0–2 indices kept by no one.
- Quotes: topic-14 ("does not support color swap on WSE-3"), game-of-life (8 colors, diagonal forwarding quote), histogram-torus "reroute", 25-pt README "21 … 6 … 4".
- Program colors: 02 streams on color 0; 03 uses c0/c1 checkerboard (snippet matches the PE (0,0)→(1,0) pair); 04 uses 4; 05 uses color 0 multicast.
- Hop table: slopes 4.0/PE (WSE-3), 2.0/PE (WSE-2), +63–64 for 63 wavelets. All consistent with CCN 20 and Ch 12.
- Arithmetic: 1,742 hops → 1.6 µs / 2.0 µs; 1,103 cycles → 1.0 / 1.3 µs; 4.4 / 3.4 GB/s per link; aggregate 2.4 × 10¹⁷ and 2.1 × 10¹⁷ vs 220 Pb/s; quiz answers 1,203 and 24 − 4 − 8 = 12.
- Clock statements follow CCN 2/10. The 750 × 994 CS-2 fabric is from the single-tile-matvec README.
- WSE-3 one-rx rule: consistent with `is_input_direction` and the 3-bit RX field.
