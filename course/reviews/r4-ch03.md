## Errors

**E1 · med · #pe-speed bullet 2 and the #wse2-wse3 row "Measured f16 multiply-add".** Still open (from r3-gaps-ch03-04 G2). The page says: "f16 reaches about 3.2 per cycle on WSE-3 and 2.0 on WSE-2 … A likely reason is that each multiply-add also reads and writes y in SRAM, so memory traffic rather than lane count sets the rate". Later measurements contradict both the rate and the reason:
- CCN 22: f16 fmach runs at 4/cycle on WSE-3.
- CCN 29 and App E: WSE-2 f16 @fmach over 1,024 elements takes 270 cycles (≈3.8/cycle) when the operands start 4 banks apart, and 526 otherwise. Memory traffic is the same in both cases.
- Ch 6 already says "3.2–4 depending on layout".

So Ch 3 contradicts Ch 6, App E and App B's copy. *Right:* "≈3.2–4 (WSE-3), ≈2–3.8 (WSE-2), set by where the operands sit in the banks (Ch 6, App E)". Tag Measured.

**E2 · med · #pe-speed bullet 1: "Treat f32 as 1-wide; WSE-3 is no faster than WSE-2 here."** This is true for multiply-add only. The page's own stride table shows a contiguous f32 `@fadds` of 256 elements in 162 cycles (≈1.6/cycle with start-up, ~2/cycle at steady state) and `@fmovs` in 92 cycles. CCN 16 gives fadds 1 vs 2 per cycle and fmovs 2 vs 4 (WSE-2 vs WSE-3). A learner who takes "1-wide" literally will mis-estimate add- and copy-heavy kernels by 2–4×. The two statements are never reconciled. *Right:* "f32 multiply-add ≈ 1/cycle on both chips; f32 add ≈ 1 (WSE-2) / 2 (WSE-3), copy ≈ 2 / 4 per cycle; each op adds ≈ 15–17 cycles of start-up (CCN 16/23)". A 3-row op-rate table would do it.

**E3 · low · #wavelets: "32-bit messages that hop one router per cycle [Indep]".** Still open (r3 G11). The WSE-3 simulator measures 2.0 cycles per hop (Ch 4, CCN 20). *Right:* "about one cycle per hop on hardware, per papers; the WSE-3 simulator models 2 (Ch 4)".

**E4 · low · #memory-map: "Its own arrays (A, x, y) are only 324 bytes" and #memory-map: "A is 43,268 bytes".** The numbers are right, but the reason is hidden: run.py allocates A as `nb*padded_nb + 1` elements and writes data at index `+1`. That is where the extra 4 bytes come from (64 + 8 + 8 = 80 f32 = 320 B, plus 4). The page lets a careful reader think the arithmetic is wrong. *Fix:* "324 bytes (A carries one leading pad element; see run.py)". This also matters for G1 below.

**E5 · low · #wse2-wse3 Clock row and #feed.** Still open (r3 G12). "Not published" stays true, but the page says nothing about the WSE-3T/CS-4, which Ch 2 discusses: same PE, analyst clock estimates. Add one footnote that links Ch 2 and is tagged Analyst/Unverified.

## Connecting gaps

**G1 · high · "f32 FMA ≈ 1/cycle" (#pe-speed) → Lab 1's "2 cycles per element" (Ch 11) and the "pad and measure" rule.** Partly still open (r3 G3), with new evidence. The page presents 1/cycle as a property of the PE. Ch 11 then shows program 01 at 2 cycles per element until a single pad word is added (CCN 22/23/29: same start bank 2,063 cycles vs 1,039 for 1,024 elements, both chips). The matvec benchmark that Ch 3 times avoids the clash on purpose: run.py pads columns to 16 B ("to avoid bank conflicts") and shifts A by one element (`+1`). Nobody tested whether the shift is what keeps it at 1/cycle, but nothing on the page connects the measurement to its layout.

*Bridge:* a 3-row box under #bank-model titled "Where two operand streams start matters". Rows: same bank / 2 banks apart / 4 banks apart. Columns: f32 fmacs and f16 fmach, on both chips, from CCN 29. Add one sentence: "the matvec numbers above were taken with the SDK's padded layout; Lab 1 shows what happens without it." Link Ch 6's explorer and App E. Effort S.

**G2 · med · vendor lane counts (#simd) → the headline PFLOPs (Ch 2).** The page gives lanes (8 f16), measured rates (1 f32, 3.2–4 f16) and a 125 PF sparse headline. It never multiplies them out. *Bridge:* a short derivation chain, built like the #feed one:
- 8 lanes × 2 FLOP × 900k PEs × ~1.08 GHz ≈ 15.6 PF, which is Ch 2's dense analyst estimate. That estimate assumes a ~1.1 GHz clock (Derived).
- At the measured 4 MAC/cycle and 850 MHz the result is ≈ 6 PF f16.
- f32 is ≈ 1.5 PF.
- The 125 PF figure is a further ~8× for assumed sparsity.

This links the PE to the system-level numbers learners will see in Ch 8, 9 and 18. Effort S.

**G3 · med · RAMP (anatomy) → CE: the input and output queues are missing from the figure and the text.** The journey's steps 3 and 5, the microthread table, "data task IDs come from input queues" and the collision story all depend on queues. Ch 5 is the first page to define queues. *Bridge:* draw a small "8 in / 6–8 out queues" block between the RAMP and the CE. Add one sentence: "hardware FIFOs bound to a color; on WSE-3 they also name the data task and default microthread". Effort S.

**G4 · med · "44.5 KB usable per PE" (#memory-map) → what a model needs (Ch 8/9).** The no-caches section ends with "data must already be in its 48 KB, or must arrive over the fabric". It never gives the wafer total that results: 900k × ~44.5 KB ≈ 40 GB usable of the 44 GB headline. It also never says that this is why weights are either pinned across many PEs or streamed (Ch 8 MemoryX, Ch 9 KV limits). *Bridge:* one derived sentence plus forward links. Effort S.
