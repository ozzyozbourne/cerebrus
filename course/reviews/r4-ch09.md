## Connecting gaps

**G1 · med · Single-PE experiment → 45,000-PE region GEMV. Effort M.**
- **The gap.** The page measures one square 140 × 140 tile and then says every PE "holds a slice of all of that layer's matrices". It never shows how a matrix-vector product is split over a 212 × 212 region: which PE holds which block, where x arrives from, and where the partial y goes. It cites "Hot Chips 2022's GEMM mechanism (Chapter 8)" but never draws the pinned version.
- **Why it matters 1: the collective count.** With 2-D blocking, x is broadcast down each column and partial sums are reduced along each row. That is exactly 2 collectives per matrix-vector product, each along one dimension (~212 hops). It justifies both the "at least 8" count and the default hop count, which the page currently calls "optimistic" without that mechanism.
- **Why it matters 2: the tile shape.** On a 212 × 212 grid, the per-PE blocks are about 39 × 39 (Q, O), 39 × 5 (K, V) and 39 × 135 (gate, up, down). They are not one 140 × 140 tile. Fitting the page's own f16 rows (N = 140 → 5,228 cycles, N = 70 → 1,394) gives ≈ N²/4.0 + 2.5·N cycles. That is ~2.5 cycles of overhead per column, so 39-element columns run at ~80% efficiency. The GEMV term could be ~10–25% above 5,200 cycles, depending on loop orientation (Derived).
- **Bridge.** Reuse Ch 8's `#lw` drawing with the weights resident: x multicast down columns, FMACs, y reduced along rows. Add a small table of per-PE block shapes for one Llama-70B layer, and one sentence on the tile-shape efficiency.

**G2 · med · Clock estimate → every budget conclusion.** This is the connecting side of E1. The "history" paragraph says "The clock did not change", and CS-4 "pulls the clock lever". With the press estimates (1.4 GHz to 2.8 GHz), the CS-4 claim of >4,400 tok/s on gpt-oss-120B can be tied to a ~2× clock ratio. The page does not make that link. Effort S, after E1.

**G3 · low-med · Ch 9 KV user limit → Ch 18's cost model. Effort S.**
- **The gap.** Ch 9 says "at most ~13 users at 8K (~7 after code)", "far below the 600,000 tok/s pipe-full ceiling". Ch 18 l.262 says that $0.60/M implies "roughly 60 concurrent streams per instance". Neither page reconciles the two.
- **Bridge.** Add one line in "Fast per user is not the same as cheap per token": the user limit scales inversely with context. At ~1–2K context, 36 GB holds roughly 50–100 sequences (~30–60 after code), so Ch 18's 60 streams implies short contexts.

**G4 · low · Decode budget → time to first token. Effort S–M.**
- **The gap.** The history table quotes TTFT (170 ms, 240 ms), and HC2024's "prompt tokens processed in parallel" is quoted. But no worked number connects prefill to the pipeline model.
- **Bridge.** A 1,000-token prompt pipelined through 80 stages takes about (1,000 + 80) stage-times at ~6 µs ≈ 6.5 ms if per-position cost stays the same. That is far below 170 ms, so TTFT is dominated by something else: queueing, host/network, or a per-position cost much higher than decode (Derived). Say so, or say it is unknown.

**G5 · low · Attention term → where a user's KV lives in the region. Effort S.**
- **The gap.** The attention line spreads KV "over the region's PEs". The collectives line needs a softmax max and sum "across the region's slices". The page never says how 8 KV heads × context are laid out over ~45,000 PEs (by head, or by context slice).
- **Bridge.** One sentence and a tiny sketch: context-sliced KV makes attention a partial-softmax reduce, which is why attention adds collectives as well as bytes.

**G6 · low · Read-port hypothesis → Ch 3 bank model.** Covered in X3. Without this link, the "8 per cycle in principle" upper bound (what-if B and the 4,000 tok/s figure) floats free of the chapters that teach the SRAM. Effort S.
