## Connecting gaps

**G1 (med, S) stored activations → "hundreds of thousands of tokens per shard".** The page says "The activations of every layer must stay on the wafer until the backward pass uses them", and also that shards "typically contain hundreds of thousands of tokens". A learner who multiplies these out gets stuck. Take GPT-3 XL (H = 2,048, 24 layers) at 250k tokens: layer outputs alone are 2,048 × 2 B × 250k ≈ 1 GB per layer, or ~25 GB for 24 layers. Full transformer intermediates are 10–30× that, far above 44 GB. The page never says how this fits: storing only layer-boundary tensors and recomputing the rest, smaller shards for wide models, or something else. Bridge: a two-line worked number plus the white paper's actual answer. If no source gives one, add an explicit "the sources do not say how activations fit at this shard size" caveat. Do not let the two statements sit side by side unreconciled.

**G2 (med, S) the 2-byte link weight → the 32-bit packet and the 4-byte sparse copy.** Three formats appear on one page with nothing tying them together. The link carries "FP16 … 32 bits each way per parameter" (2 B per crossing). The fabric packet is "weight (16b) + index (16b)". MemoryX stores a "sparse working copy: FP16 value + 2-byte column index". The reader cannot tell whether a sparse model sends 4 B per non-zero over the link, or where the index is attached. Bridge: one sentence stating which format crosses the link (dense: 2 B; sparse: value + index per non-zero, so sparsity cuts link bytes only when more than half the weights are zero), and that the index addresses the target column on the wafer.

**G3 (med, M) Y = X·W → AXPY down a column plus a row reduction.** #layer describes the multicast, FMAC, PSUM and FSUM correctly (checked against HC22: "Row of non-zero weights broadcast over columns of cores", "Partial sums reduced over rows of cores in a ring", "FSUM command directs final sums to the correct column"). It never shows why a row reduction is needed. Bridge: a 2 × 2 worked example. Weight W[i][k] goes to the column holding input feature k. Each core there adds W[i][k]·act[j][k] into its partial sum for output i. Partial sums for output i therefore sit in every column, and that is why each row must ring-reduce them and FSUM must deposit the result in output i's column. Then add the CSL mapping in one line each: a color route with several outputs (Ch04), a data task or `@fmach` with a fabric-input DSD (Ch05/06), and PSUM/FSUM as control wavelets (Ch04/05).

**G4 (low, S) Ch01's off-wafer ridge → Ch08's knee.** Ch01's roofline gives the CS-3 off-wafer ridge as "≈ 100,000" FLOP/B, and Ch08 derives "about T FLOP per byte". These are the same number: the knee in tokens per shard equals the ridge, which the widget confirms at about 104k for WSE-3. Saying so in one sentence would turn two separate facts into a single idea that runs through the course.

**G5 (low, S) "tokens per shard" → sequences.** The 50,000-token knee is never put in model terms. Add "≈ 25 sequences of 2,048 tokens per wafer", so that "batch shard" and "batch size" connect.

**G6 (med, S; the fix is in Ch09) Ch09 still cites content that has left Ch08.** Ch09 #budget says "Chapter 8 ended with 'the sources do not break this down'", and the experiment box says "The f32 result reproduces Chapter 8's benchmark run (10,160 cycles)". Neither phrase nor any benchmark run remains in Ch08: the GEMV experiment and the decode widgets moved to Ch09, as the "moved from Chapter 8" comments in its scripts show. A learner who follows these back-references finds nothing. Fix: reword both in Ch09 as self-contained statements.

**G7 (low, M) still open (from ch08-fixlog, D5).** There is no SwarmX tree diagram. "Node count grows linearly … latency grows as log N" and "1:4 or two 1:2" would be much clearer as a 3-level tree drawing (MemoryX at the root, broadcast–reduce nodes, CS-3 leaves).

**G8 (low, S) training basics.** No earlier chapter introduces forward pass, backward pass, gradient, loss or optimizer step, and this page relies on them. A 3-sentence "a training step in one paragraph" box before #weight-streaming (or a glossary link) would carry readers who come from HPC rather than ML. X2 and X3 would then need fewer words.
