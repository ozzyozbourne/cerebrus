# r4 audit · ch17 (17-gpu.html, "NVIDIA GPUs, the incumbent")

**Verdict: Mostly correct. Explanation quality: good.** The arithmetic is sound: I re-derived every worked number and widget formula, and a Playwright run at 1100 px showed 0 console errors with readouts that match the prose. What remains is two framing errors, one claim that contradicts Ch 6, several r3 items still open, and missing bridges back to Ch 4, 9 and 14 and to App B.

## Errors

**E1 · med · "Three generations", "three moves" paragraph.** The page says: "HBM grows ~5.7× from H100 to Rubin, far less than the FLOPs, so FLOPs per byte keeps rising and the memory wall gets worse each generation." This only holds when you compare BF16 against FP4. At equal precision the ridge stays about flat. H100 BF16 is 989 TF ÷ 3.35 TB/s ≈ 295 FLOP/B. B200 BF16 is 2.25 PF ÷ 8 TB/s ≈ 281 (both values come from the page's own table). Counting per *value* read gives the same picture: B200 FP4 is 9 PF ÷ (8 TB/s × 2 values/B) ≈ 560 FLOP/value, against H100 BF16 at ≈ 590. The ratio rises only for Rubin's 50 PF "inference" figure, which depends on compression (≈ 1,300; ≈ 910 at the 35 PF training figure). *Fix:* "At the same precision the ridge is roughly flat (295 → ~281 for B200); the headline growth comes from narrower formats, and Rubin's NVFP4 inference figure is the first to raise FLOPs per value read clearly." [derived]

**E2 · med · "Coalescing: count the sectors", closing sentence.** The page says: "On a PE, a strided DSD costs nothing extra, because SRAM is single-cycle and accessed per element (Chapter 6)." Ch 6 ("bank view") says the opposite. The 48 KB is 8 single-ported banks interleaved by word, and stride-31 columns took 1,294 instead of 1,078 cycles (+20%, measured). Notes 22, 23 and 29 report up to 2× from operand bank clashes. *Fix:* "a strided DSD costs no wasted bytes; only bank clashes between operand streams can slow it (Chapter 6, up to 2×)."

**E3 · low-med · "One token, step by step".** The page says: "attention stays near 1 FLOP/B at any batch." That is true for multi-head attention. Llama-3.1-70B uses GQA with 64 query heads per 8 KV heads, so each KV element serves 8 query heads: ≈ 8 FLOP/B with a BF16 KV cache and ≈ 16 with FP8. The conclusion still holds, because that is far below the 295 ridge, but the number does not match the model the page names. *Fix:* "~8 FLOP/B for Llama-70B's 8-way GQA (1 for plain multi-head attention), still far below the ridge." [derived]

**E4 · low · "Where the 989 TF comes from".** The 989 TF figure is derived at 1.83 GHz. The "about 67 TF FP32" figure implies 132 × 128 × 2 × **1.98 GHz**; at 1.83 GHz it would be ≈ 62 TF. A learner who checks one figure with the other's clock gets a mismatch. *Fix:* add a half-sentence: "the FP32 figure is quoted at the 1.98 GHz max boost; the Tensor Core figure corresponds to 1.83 GHz" [derived], or tag the clock basis Unverified.

**E5 · low · "Tiling a matmul".** The page says: "GEMM still hits peak because neighbouring blocks re-read the same slabs from the 50 MB L2". Two problems:
- At 128 FLOP/B, peak needs ≈ 989/128 ≈ 7.7 TB/s from L2, which is above the page's own ">5.5 TB/s" figure. So L2 reuse alone does not close the gap on the page's numbers. Cluster/TMA multicast, larger effective tiles and the SXM part's higher L2 bandwidth are part of the story.
- Real cuBLAS/CUTLASS BF16 GEMMs reach roughly 70–80% of 989 TF, not 100%. This could not be re-verified here, so tag it Unverified if used.

*Fix:* "approaches peak", plus one sentence that runs this second roofline check against L2.

**E6 · low · Scaling-out worked example.** "Train a 70B model with TP = 8 on one HGX H100" cannot fit: with Adam, about 16 B/param ≈ 1.1 TB of state against 640 GB of HBM. *Fix:* "one TP = 8 group of a larger job (states sharded by FSDP/PP)". The communication arithmetic itself is right.

**E7 · still open (from r3-gaps-ch16-17 G1) · high (visible inconsistency).** The SM/PE/Tensix table gives Blackhole as "~1.43 MiB SRAM (1,464 KiB)". Ch 16, Ch 18 and App A/B all give 1,536 KiB (1,464 is Wormhole).

**E8 · still open (r3 G6, G7, G13).**
- The Rubin column still says "not published in our sources" for BF16 dense and gives no shipping status.
- The LPX section still says "256 … 128 … not settled here", although Ch 18 repeats the same hedge and r3 found 256 well supported and production status announced.
- B300/GB300 is still absent.

**E9 · still open (r3 G8).** There are 13 `tag unv` spans. The Megatron-LM papers are primary sources, so they should be tagged Indep, not Unverified. The NVL72 tray layout (18 × 4 GPUs, 9 NVSwitch trays), TMEM 256 KB and setmaxnreg 40/232 all match public CUTLASS/NVIDIA material and could be retagged once a source is reached.

## Explanation problems

**X1 · "Latency hiding by occupancy" vs the Little's-law callout.** There are two models: scheduler busy-fraction W·C/(C+L), and bytes in flight. They give different answers ("16 warps hide it" vs "~100 outstanding loads per SM") and nothing says which question each one answers. *Fix:* one sentence: "The widget asks whether the scheduler always has an instruction to issue; Little's law asks whether enough bytes are requested to fill HBM. A kernel can pass the first and fail the second."

**X2 · "Three generations" table.** "Inference figure relies on Transformer Engine compression" appears before the callout, and the callout describes the Transformer Engine only as picking FP8 scales. "Compression" is never explained. *Fix:* move the clause into the callout and say what it is (or tag it Unverified).

**X3 · Little's-law callout.** "cp.async, TMA" is used here, but TMA is defined only in the later Hopper section (and in a widget tooltip), and cp.async is never defined. *Fix:* "asynchronous copies into shared memory (cp.async on Ampere, the TMA engine on Hopper, below)".

**X4 · "Inside an SM".** "Reads one operand from shared memory" is vague. B always comes from shared memory, and A comes from registers or shared memory. *Fix:* say so in one clause.

**X5 · "One token", final paragraph.** "Four CS-3 wafers keep the weights in SRAM and pipeline layers … which is how Cerebras reaches 2,314 tokens/s" overstates the cause. Ch 9's budget needs optimised GEMV and collectives *and* speculative decoding to reach ~2,300; the measured kernel alone gives ~500 (What-if A). *Fix:* "…which makes 2,314 tok/s possible; Chapter 9 shows which kernel and speculation changes it took."

**X6 · Missing tag.** "GPUs win back on cost per token at large batch" has no tag and no number. Tag it Derived with a pointer to Ch 18, or soften it.

## Connecting gaps

**G1 · high · GPU decode bound → what GPUs actually achieve → why the wafer differs (Ch 9 / Ch 18).** The page derives ~190 tok/s for 8 × H100 but never gives the measured 86 tok/s (HC2024/AA; quoted in Ch 9 §"The GPU at batch 1" and Ch 18). It also never links to Ch 9, where the single-user wafer budget lives (notes 25–27). All three Cerebras pointers go to Ch 8 (still open from r3 G11). *Bridge:* add "measured: 86 tok/s, ~45% of the bound" and change "(Chapter 8)" to "(Chapters 8–9; Ch 9 builds the per-token budget)". S.

**G2 · med · GPU latency ladder → wafer latency.** The hierarchy figure gives cycles for every GPU level, but the wafer column says only "hop by hop". The course has measured numbers: 2.0 cycles per router hop on WSE-3 / 1.0 on WSE-2, and ~9 / 6 cycles through a PE's compute (Ch 4, notes 20 and 24). With those numbers a learner can see something the page never says. A neighbour is ~2 cycles away, but a PE ~400 hops away is as far as HBM (~800 cycles). That is why wafer layout is a locality problem in the same way GPU tiling is. *Bridge:* add the cycles to the figure plus one sentence. S.

**G3 · med · CUDA's runtime grid → CSL's compile-time layout.** The concept table maps `<<<grid, block>>>` to `@set_rectangle` + `rt.launch`. It never says that the rectangle and `N` are compile-time params in program 01 (`param N: i16`), so a new n means a recompile, and that N is capped by about 44,500 B per PE. *Bridge:* add a wafer version of the n = 1,000,000 example. Two f32 arrays need 8 MB; at ~5,500 elements per PE (≈ 44 KB ÷ 8 B) that is ≥ ~182 PEs. @fmacs runs at ~1 element/cycle with padding (note 22), so ~5,500 cycles ≈ 6.5 µs at 850 MHz, and host I/O dominates, as Lab 1's 7,740 cycles show. Set this beside the 3,907 blocks / 3.7 waves. S–M.

**G4 · med · GPU GEMM (bounded by feeding the Tensor Cores) → wafer GEMM (bounded by per-PE instruction efficiency).** The tiling section ends with "the wafer places compute next to data" (Chapter 14), but it never quotes Lab 4's finding, which Ch 18 does: the fabric takes ~2% of each step, and the SDK loop reaches 32% of one f32 FMAC/cycle (51% after a rewrite). Without that, a learner concludes the wafer GEMM is "solved". *Bridge:* one sentence with these measured numbers. S.

**G5 · low-med · SM ≈ 256 PEs → measured PE rate.** Still open from r3 G12. The per-clock equivalence uses the 8-wide vendor SIMD; the measured f16 FMAC rate is ~3.2–4 per cycle (notes 14 and 22, App B row "warp"), which makes it ≈ 512 PEs per SM in practice. *Bridge:* add a half-sentence linking to Ch 6. S.

**G6 · low · Page → App B.** App B is the full CUDA → CSL → Metalium mapping. It even maps thread-block clusters/DSMEM to "a neighbour PE at 2.0 cycles", a nuance this page lacks; this page says only "no equivalent". The page never links to it. *Bridge:* a "Full mapping: Appendix B" link under the concept table. S.

**G7 · low · Microthreads → tasks (wafer side of the latency table).** "A PE also has a few dozen task IDs" is vague. Ch 5 gives the real budget: control IDs 0–63, with user local IDs recommended at 8–20 (note 12). *Bridge:* quote it, so the "8 vs 2,048" contrast is fully numeric. S.

**G8 · low · carried over (ch14-fixlog D4/V4, r3 G14).** The Hopper pipeline is still a static figure with no ping-pong vs cooperative toggle, and there are no portrait SVG layouts. M–L.
