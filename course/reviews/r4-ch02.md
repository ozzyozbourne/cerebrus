# R4 audit · ch02 (02-the-wafer.html)

**Verdict: Mostly correct. Explanation quality: good.**

Method: I read the full page, including the widget JS (GEN table, drawPE, yield and defect code), r3-gaps-ch01-02.md, ch02-fixlog.md and CROSS_CHAPTER_NOTES 1–29. I recomputed every number on the page. I checked the SDK sources it cites (`single-tile-matvec/{run.py,README.rst,sweep.py}`, `bandwidth-test/run.appliance.py`, and the 850 MHz constants in the benchmarks), checked ch03's bank text, and grepped ch01 and the glossary for shared terms. ## Errors

**E1 · low · "One wafer, 900,000 small machines", paragraph after the calculator.** The text says "about 48 KB of memory that can serve about 7 words a cycle, roughly 9 words a cycle of mesh links … fabric bandwidth sits between … 32 B/cycle and 40 B/cycle". These hold only for WSE-3 at the slider's default 850 MHz, and the paragraph never says so. At the only published clock (WSE-2, 1.1 GHz) the same widget gives 21.4 B (5.3 words) of memory and 29.4 B of fabric, which is *below* the 32 B "lower bound". The same applies to "What a real kernel gets": "roughly 40% of the ~7". At 1.1 GHz that becomes 3 / 5.3 ≈ 56%. **Fix:** write "at 850 MHz" in both places, and say the fabric check only brackets the clock. It does not confirm it.

**E2 · low · "What a real kernel gets", lesson 1.** The text reads: "uses about 3 words per cycle, roughly 40% of the ~7 … because the vendor peak assumes the full 16-bit SIMD path". The cause given is hand-waved and does not add up. An 8-wide f16 FMAC would need 3 operands × 8 × 2 B = 48 B/cycle, well above the ~27 B the vendor memory total implies. The real link is the measured f16 rate. @fmach runs at about 4 elements/cycle on WSE-3 (note 22), which is 3 × 4 × 2 B = 24 B ≈ 6 words: roughly the vendor per-PE memory figure. **Fix:** replace the "because" clause with that arithmetic. See G2.

**E3 · low · "I/O lives on the rim" and the bandwidth ladder.** "Through the SDK's host channels, about 16 × 7 Gb/s ≈ 112 Gb/s gets in … refilling all 44 GB that way would take about 3 s." The source, the `bandwidth-test/run.appliance.py` docstring, says one channel tops out at "around 7Gbps" and the host connects "via 100Gbps ethernets". `--channels` is capped at 16. So 112 Gb/s is the memcpy channel limit one program gets. It is not the wafer's physical I/O: a CS-2 or CS-3 system has 12 × 100 GbE = 1.2 Tb/s (r3 G5). The paragraph then compares this figure with the CS-4's 2.4 Tb/s *system* figure, which mixes the two kinds of number. **Fix:** give the CS-2/CS-3 system I/O as 1.2 Tb/s [Vendor] next to the CS-4's 2.4 Tb/s. Call 112 Gb/s "what the SDK memcpy path delivers (per the bandwidth-test comment)". Note that a refill at system rate takes about 0.3 s, not 3 s.

**E4 · low · stat tile, table and quiz Q6: "750 × 994 fabric".** The SDK does not agree with itself here. The single-tile-matvec README says that on a CS-2 "`fabric-dims` must be `750,994`". The same README tells you to run `./sweep.py --dims 750,994` as the program rectangle, and `sweep.py` line 33 then requests `--fabric-dims={width+7},{height+2}` = 757 × 996. `run.py` also scales results "to program rectangle" 750 × 994. The course's reading is defensible but is not the only one. **Fix:** add a footnote that the SDK benchmark scripts also treat 750 × 994 as the program size, so the exact CS-2 fabric could be 757 × 996. Keep the 750 × 994 / 743 × 992 numbers, attributed to the README sentence.

**E5 · low · still open (from r3-gaps-ch01-02 G10).** These tags were not updated: NVLink "0.9 TB/s Vendor (not re-verified)", GH100 "144 … Industry figure (not re-verified)", H100 die "~814 mm² Industry figure" and "~700 W … Industry figures". ch17 and ch18 tag 900 GB/s and 700 W as Vendor. **Fix:** retag them consistently.

**E6 · low · source naming.** "HC34: 50:50 logic to SRAM" and "Vendor (HC22)" both refer to the same Hot Chips 2022 talk under two names. ch03 calls it "Hot Chips 2022". **Fix:** use "HC22" everywhere and gloss it once as "Hot Chips 2022 (HC34)".

## Explanation problems

**X1 · small-machines callout.** The 12N² + 4N byte count is stated but never derived, so the learner cannot see why it comes to 3 words per FMA. **Fix:** add one clause: "each step reads A[i,j] and y[i] and writes y[i]: 3 × 4 B per multiply-add (x is read once per column)". The measured 12 B/cycle then follows directly.

**X2 · fabric row of the calculator.** "4 links × 2 directions × 4 B" counts each link once from each endpoint, so the wafer total counts every physical link twice. A learner who multiplies 32 B/cycle by 900k and compares it with traffic will over-estimate by 2×. **Fix:** add one sentence: "vendor fabric totals sum router ports, so each link is counted at both ends".

**X3 · "Read the FLOPs" and the SC20 row.** "sustained about 0.86 PF, roughly a third of peak": no CS-1 peak appears anywhere on the page (the table has "—"). **Fix:** state the peak the paper uses, or drop "a third of peak".

**X4 · repair caption.** The caption uses "PE slots with a router", but the router has not been introduced. The page defined PE = core earlier and never splits a PE into compute plus router. **Fix:** add a clause in the reticle section: "each PE pairs a compute core with a 5-port router (Chapter 4)".

**X5 · "Where the 850k cores go" caption.** "--fabric-offsets=4,1" and "--channels 1" are raw compiler flags. The learner meets them eight chapters later (Ch 7, Ch 10). **Fix:** gloss them inline: "(the program rectangle starts 4 columns in and 1 row down; one I/O channel)".

**X6 · whole page.** The page has no hyperlinks to other chapters or to the glossary, and every "Chapter N" reference is plain text. Appendix A has entries for wavelet, router and fabric. **Fix:** link the first use of each term to the glossary and each "Chapter N" to its page. This is low priority; ch01 and ch04 also have no links.

## Connecting gaps

**G1 · med · "fabric / links" → "wavelet".** "A wavelet moves about one hop per cycle" is the first use of *wavelet* in the course: ch01 never uses the word and ch02 does not define it. The "32-bit links" and "4 B" per link in the calculator rest on the same undefined unit. **Bridge:** in the reticle section, after "fabric", add: "the fabric moves data in *wavelets*, 32-bit packets tagged with a colour that picks their route (Chapter 4)". Effort S.

**G2 · med · still open (from r3-gaps-ch01-02 G4): vendor SIMD width → what the SDK achieves.** The first-principles check, the calculator and quiz Q5 all use the full 8-wide (WSE-3) and 4-wide (WSE-2) f16 SIMD widths. The measured f16 rates (@fmach about 4/cycle on WSE-3 and 2/cycle on WSE-2, notes 14/22; ch09 says 8-wide is "not demonstrated") are missing. The learner leaves with "12–16 PF dense" and no measured counterpart. **Bridge:** add a calculator bar "measured f16 kernel rate (Ch 6)" at 4 FMAC/cycle, which gives ≈ 6.1 PF at 850 MHz. Add one sentence to the callout tying 4 × 3 × 2 B ≈ 24 B/cycle to the vendor memory total (this also fixes E2). Effort S.

**G3 · low · still open (from r3 G8): hop latency → the labs.** The text still says "about one hop per cycle" and gives crossing times only at 1 cycle/hop. Ch04 and note 20 measure 2.0 cycles/hop on the WSE-3 simulator, and 1.0 on WSE-2. **Bridge:** add "(the WSE-3 simulator models 2 cycles per hop, Chapter 4; double the times there)". Effort S.

**G4 · low · "per-PE budget" → "what CSL code sees".** The page promises that "every later chapter works at that scale", but it never links the budget rows to concrete program limits. The 48 KB row should say that about 44 KB is usable after the fixed code and task-table overhead (note 13). The FLOP row should say that a single DSD op (`@fmacs`) is the unit that reaches about 1 element/cycle. **Bridge:** add a "Means later" column entry: "≈ 44 KB for your arrays (Ch 3); one @fmacs ≈ 1 elem/cycle (Ch 6)". Effort S.

**G5 · low · "I/O on the rim" → "weights stay resident (Ch 7–8)".** The page jumps from "3 s to refill" to "weights stay resident or are streamed" without saying what that means for a model bigger than 44 GB. **Bridge:** add a forward pointer of one sentence: "models larger than 44 GB stream weights in from external memory layer by layer (MemoryX, Chapter 8), which is why the off-wafer bandwidth figure matters for training". Effort S.

## Checked and fine
- 46,225 mm² gives a 215 mm side and a 304 mm diagonal; 65% of the wafer is used, ~24,000 mm² of corners; 57× an H100; perimeter 860 mm.
- Poisson numbers: e^−0.858 ≈ 42%, e^−46 ≈ 1.0 × 10⁻²⁰, 46 of 900k = 0.005%. Birthday estimates: 65% (994 bins) and 12% (7,952 bins).
- 164× accounting: 2.2 × 164 / 46 ≈ 7.8 mm²; 2.2 / 46 ≈ 0.048 mm². Core-area shares: 97% and 71%.
- FLOPs: 7.48 / 15.84 / 12.24 PF; H100 dense 989 TF is half of the 1,979 TF figure; WSE-3 is 2.08× WSE-2.
- Bandwidth: 26.8 PB/s ÷ 0.9 TB/s ≈ 29,700×; fabric ÷ 112 Gb/s ≈ 1.9 M; hop times 0.68 / 1.59 / 0.88 / 2.05 µs.
- single-tile-matvec: 12N² + 4N = 120,400 B and 2N² − N FLOPs, per run.py lines 223–225. 11.9 B/cycle, ~1 FMA/cycle, 7.51 PB/s and 1.24 PF all match the script's 850 MHz × 750 × 994 scaling. An 8 × 3 fabric with a 1 × 1 rectangle fits the 7/2 rule. 40 KB of A matches the padding.

- Halo/memcpy frame: 4 + 3 columns, "at least" 7 × 2, 743 × 992 = 737,056, and the 3 W / 2 E measurement all match notes 8/9 and ch07.

- SRAM per PE (18 GB/400k, 40 GB/850k, 44 GB/900k) is consistent with 48 KB. The clock story matches notes 2/10.
