# R4 audit · ch06 (06-dsds.html, "DSDs: loops as hardware")

**Verdict: Mostly correct. Explanation quality: good.** The SDK quotes, program excerpts, the dsd_ops family matrix, the worked bytes/FLOPs arithmetic, the matvec tables and the quiz answers all check out. The main problems are three things that contradict other pages: the bank model, the f16 rate on WSE-2, and the cost of a scalar-bodied `@map`. The biggest connecting gap is that the page never explains why the collision rule is "mod 4" when its own bank picture is "mod 8".

Prior reports (ch06-accuracy/depth/pedagogy/visuals/fixlog): everything they list as fixed is fixed. Not re-reported.

## Errors

**E1 (med) · #collisions, first paragraph, and the visualizer's "bank view (word mod 8)".**
- The page says: "A PE's 48 KB is 8 single-ported banks of 6 KB, interleaved by 32-bit word `Vendor`."
- Chapter 3 (#bank-model) and Appendix E (issue 1) attribute the 6 KB size and the 32-bit interleave to academic papers (Independent), not to the vendor. The SDK's own rule is different. The cs_readelf man page says "bits [3:1] of the address", i.e. bank = (byte address ÷ 2) mod 8, and `--sym` output confirms it. Under that rule one f32 spans two banks and the 8 banks repeat every 4 words.
- The page's own empirical rule ("equal modulo 4" words) only makes sense under the SDK model. The visualizer's word-mod-8 bank view uses the other model.
- Fix: tag the 8 single-ported banks Vendor (HC22), and the 6 KB and 32-bit interleave Independent. State the SDK 16-bit rule. Either relabel the bank view as "word mod 4 = SDK start bank" or offer both models, as Chapter 3's explorer does.

**E2 (med) · #op-speed, rate table and "Precision is where SIMD width pays off".**
- The page says: "f16 FMAC runs at about 2 per cycle on WSE-2" and "memory traffic, not arithmetic, appears to set the f16 speed `Derived`". The collision rule is also stated with no precision qualifier.
- Appendix E (#iss-clash, measured with cs_readelf placement) found that f16 `@fmach` on WSE-2 takes 270 cycles for 1,024 elements, about 4 per cycle (the full SIMD width), when the two sources start 4 banks apart. It takes 526 cycles at 0 or 2 banks apart. So on WSE-2 f16 speed is set by layout, and the f32 "mod 4 = slow" rule is reversed there. On WSE-3 f16 is 272 cycles at all three offsets.
- Fix: in the `@fmach` row, give WSE-2 as "2, or 4 with sources 8 bytes apart (App E)". Scope the collision rule to f32. Soften the memory-traffic sentence for WSE-2.

**E3 (low) · #stencil, last paragraph.**
- The page says: "at 2.5 cycles per cell … the three instructions cost 40 cycles for n = 16 on WSE-3."
- Chapter 13 measures 59 cycles: 40 cycles of streaming plus about 19 of start-up (about 6 per instruction). The page itself says start-up dominates short vectors.
- Fix: "40 cycles of streaming plus about 19 of start-up ≈ 59 (Chapter 13)."

**E4 (low) · #op-speed, "A fixed start-up of about 15–17 cycles per op".**
- This is stated as a general property, and the page concludes that vectors shorter than about 16 elements are dominated by start-up.
- Chapter 13 measures about 6 cycles (WSE-3) and about 5 (WSE-2) per instruction for back-to-back ops inside a task. Its own text notes that this disagrees with Chapter 6.
- Fix: add "for an isolated op bracketed by timestamp reads; consecutive ops in one task start in about 6 (Chapter 13)".

**E5 (low) · #builtins, the table intro.**
- The page says: "The four builtins the course's programs use:". The table has five rows, and the "Used in" column stops at program 05.
- Program 06 (SUMMA) uses a two-DSD `@fmuls(T2, A2, B2)` and `@fadds`. Program 07 (CG) uses `@fsubs`, `@map` with a scalar accumulator, and `@set_dsd_base_addr`.
- Fix: say "five", and add programs 06 and 07 to the "Used in" column (and the `@fsubs` row).

**E6 (low) · #one-instruction (the opening example) and the scalar-vs-DSD widget.**
- The widget and readout give "≈ 17 + N cycles" for program 01's `@fmacs`.
- Program 01 as shipped puts x and y back to back. With N a multiple of 4 this is exactly the colliding layout: 2 cycles per element, measured in Chapter 11 (#kernel-time) and noted in CCN 22.
- Fix: add a footnote, "as shipped, program 01 hits the collision below: 2/element until padded". See G2.

**E7 (low) · lede.** The page says "With no caches and no DRAM". Chapter 3 lists a 256 B (WSE-2) and 512 B (WSE-3) software-managed cache. Fix: write "no hardware caches".

## Explanation problems

**X1 · #op-speed rate table.** The "elements / cycle" column is net of start-up, but the table does not say so. A reader who divides 1,024 by 310 gets 3.3, not "≈3.5"; for `@fmovh`, 1,024 / 162 gives 6.3, not "≈7". Fix: add "(after subtracting ~17 cycles of start-up)" to the column header.

**X2 · #op-speed method paragraph.** It relies on "the PE's timestamp counter … `<time>` module" without a one-line gloss. TSC timing is taught later, in Chapter 11 and Appendix C. Fix: one sentence ("a per-PE cycle counter read by `<time>`; Chapter 11 shows how") and a pointer.

**X3 · #fifos, "stalls when size exceeds 14".** The page states the number but not the mechanism. The pipeline-01 README gives it: H2D and D2H are serialized, so the output cannot drain until the H2D finishes. The internal queues fill, back-pressure stops the input, and the H2D stalls. Without this, "14" reads as a queue depth, which the page itself says it is not. Fix: paraphrase the README and link back to Chapter 4's back-pressure.

**X4 · Two different bank pictures on one page.** The collision widget colours words mod 4, while the visualizer colours them mod 8, and neither says why. This is fixed by E1 together with G1.

**X5 · Forward references before Chapter 7.** "H2D", "D2H", "memcpy kernel" (in the async section and the timeline) and "halo PE" (in the FIFO table) are used with no gloss. Fix: a short parenthetical for each, plus "Chapter 7".

**X6 · Cross-references.** "Chapter N" appears 16 times with no hyperlinks, and no term links to the glossary. This is the same across the course, so it is low priority. Adding anchors would help most in #collisions (to Ch 3 #bank-model and App E #iss-clash) and in the microthread table (to Ch 5's collision explorer).

## Connecting gaps

**G1 (high, S) · The bank layout (Ch 3) does not connect to the "equal mod 4 words" collision rule (here).**
- The page states the rule as purely empirical and gives no mechanism.
- Bridge: two sentences plus a small strip figure. "The SDK numbers banks in 16-bit units (cs_readelf: bits [3:1]), so an f32 word covers banks 2k and 2k+1, and four words cover all eight banks. Two streams whose start addresses are equal mod 4 words therefore start in the same bank and keep hitting the same banks every cycle. That is the man page's 'same bank' case."
- Also quote the man-page sentence ("same bank or the same bank modulo 4 may result in cycle delays"). Note that App E found its second clause does not slow down f32.

**G2 (med, S) · The opening SAXPY example does not connect to #collisions.**
- The chapter's first program is the textbook collision. Chapter 11 measures it at half speed, but this page never says so. The learner meets the 2× effect in Lab 1 with no link back to here.
- Bridge: in #collisions, add "Program 01 as shipped: x and y adjacent, N = 1,024 → 2,063 cycles; one padding word → 1,039 (Chapter 11)". Turn quiz question 5 into a question about program 01.

**G3 (med, M) · "@map is the fast path" (#dsrs) does not connect to Lab 5's `@map` dot product (about 10 cycles per cell, Chapter 15).**
- The page shows only `@map` whose body issues a DSR-based `@fmacs`. That gives N + 2 per column because each call is one vector instruction.
- Program 07 uses `@map(dot_step, u_dsd, v_dsd, &s, &s)` with a scalar body. Chapter 15 measures it at about 10 cycles per element, and the learner cannot predict that from Chapter 6.
- Bridge: explain the signature (`fn`, input DSDs or scalars, then the output), and that `@map` calls `fn` once per element. It is fast per element only when `fn` itself issues a vector op over many elements. Contrast the two uses in a two-row table, and point to Chapter 15.

**G4 (med, S) · Fabric back-pressure (Ch 4) does not connect to fabric DSD op behaviour (here).**
- The page never says what an async fabric op does when its input queue is empty or its output link is backed up: it stalls per element on its microthread, while the CE and other microthreads go on.
- This one fact explains three things on the page: why sync fabric ops deadlock, why "completion" means the data left the output queue, and the 14-wavelet stall (X3).
- Bridge: one paragraph at the top of #fabric-dsds that links back to Chapter 4.

**G5 (low-med, S) · "The CE has no resource to run the H2D command" does not connect to the memcpy runtime running on each PE (Ch 7).**
- The timeline widget shows a "memcpy kernel handler" running on the CE, but the prose never says that memcpy is code on each PE's CE. Without that, the pipeline-01 quote is puzzling.
- Bridge: one sentence, "memcpy is not a DMA engine: its receive handler runs as tasks on this PE's CE (Chapter 7)".

**G6 (low, S) · The GEMV column sweep (here) does not connect to SUMMA's inner loop (Lab 4).**
- Program 06's `@fmacs(C_dsd, C_dsd, A_dsd, Bp.*[j*Kt+k])` is the same pattern of "1-D DSD × scalar, move, repeat".
- Bridge: one forward sentence after the GEMV widget.

**G7 (low, M) · Still open (from ch06-fixlog, "Left for later"):**

- no mini diagram for `wavelet_index_offset`, and the `.index` unit is still "Derived";
