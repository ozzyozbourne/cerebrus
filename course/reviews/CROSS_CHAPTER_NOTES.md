15. **Blackhole numbers (ch01, ch15).** L1 1536 KiB (Wormhole 1464). NoC flits 64 B, packets ≤16 KiB (Wormhole 32 B / 8 KiB). NoC at 1.35 GHz. Up to 64 circular buffers. 16. **DSD op rates (ch03, ch10–12, ch15).** In the simulator (memory to memory, f32): @fmacs/@fmuls 1 element/cycle on both chips; @fadds 1/cycle on WSE-2 and 2 on WSE-3; @fmovs 2 and 4; about 15 cycles to start each op. 17. **Timing noise (ch09, ch12, ch15).** Whole-run sim_stats.json cycles vary with host load (+1–4%, sometimes more), and loaded runs can abort with "could be a kernel stall". In-kernel `<time>` TSC numbers are stable. Do NOT claim "±2 cycles". Recommend TSC for kernel timing. The per-cycle instruction trace is available via SINGULARITYENV_SIMFABRIC_DEBUG=inst_trace. Source: reviews/ch10-depth.md.
18. **Program 01 cost split.** @fmacs ≈130 cycles (2/elem f32); each H2D ≈1,360; D2H ≈4,400 of 7,740. Totals: WSE-3 ≈ 3,640 + 64·N. Source: reviews/ch10-depth.md.
19. **Timing across PEs (ch11, ch12, ch15).** TSC values on different PEs are not comparable in the simulator: each counter starts when the launch reaches that PE. Time on one PE, or use a round trip.
20. **Hop cost (ch11, ch15).** A pure router hop measured as a round trip on one PE costs 2.0 cycles/hop on WSE-3 and 1.0 on WSE-2, plus 1 cycle per extra wavelet on WSE-3. A WSE-3 route accepts at most 1 input direction (compile error with 2). Source: ch04 page and ch04-fixlog.md.

25. **Single-user vs aggregate decode bound (supersedes part of note 6).** 4 × CS-3 = 84 PB/s → ~600,000 tok/s is the aggregate over all users in the layer pipeline. One user's token occupies one of 80 layer regions at a time, so the single-user bound is ~1/80: ~7,500 tok/s at headline bandwidth, ~2,000 at the measured f16 matvec rate (7.5 B/cycle/PE, Ch 9). Measured 2,314 tok/s is ~30% of the headline single-user bound. Say "per-region SRAM reads plus latency/communication", not "bandwidth is almost free". Source: reviews/build-ch09.md.
26. **Why TSC values differ across PEs (refines note 19).** `<time>`'s enable_tsc() zeroes the counter, so the offset between two PEs is the moment each one called it (when the launch reached it), not a random start. Still: only subtract timestamps from the same PE. Whole-run cycle_count noise can be large: −3% to +41% seen on reruns of the Appendix C example. Source: reviews/r2-appC-accuracy.md.
27. **Single-user decode range (refines 25).** At the measured f16 matvec rate (7.5 B/cycle/PE ≈ 4 MAC/cycle; a kernel limit, WSE-3 is 8-wide f16 per HC2024) one user gets ~2,000 tok/s; at a not-demonstrated 8-wide kernel ~4,000. The ~7,500 headline overstates (21 PB/s counts writes). Measured 2,314 sits in the range; GEMV is half to all of the per-layer budget. Frame as a dependency chain of 80 layer reads plus latency. Source: reviews/r2-ch09-fixlog.md.
28. **Speculative decoding on a wafer.** Draft cost c ≈ 0.3–0.4 (draft is latency-bound too) → ~1.4× vs ~2.8× on GPU; verification is pipelined (+k/S). KV-limited: at 8K at most ~13 users (~7 after ~4.2 KB/PE code), so ≤13 of 80 pipe slots busy, speculation nearly free system-wide; total ≤ ~27k tok/s per 4 × CS-3 at 8K. Say "at most 13 users". 4-bit weights + 16-bit compute buy capacity, not per-token speed. The 405B 969 → 539 tok/s drop at 100K context is not explained by KV reads (~6%/layer). Source: reviews/r2-ch09-fixlog.md.
29. **cs_readelf "same bank modulo 4" test (App E open issue 2).** f32 @fmacs, 1,024 elems: same-bank start 2,063 (WSE-3) / 2,062 (WSE-2) cycles; 2 or 4 banks apart 1,039 / 1,038. f16 @fmach: WSE-2 same 526, 2 apart 526, 4 apart 270; WSE-3 272 in all three. The "modulo 4" offset costs nothing in f32; on WSE-2 f16 only the 4-apart case is fast. Source: reviews/r2-appE-fixlog.md.

## Round 4 (coordinator decisions; supersede earlier items where they conflict)

30. **Operand placement sets DSD op rates (supersedes the rate parts of 14, 22, 23, 29).** SRAM is 8 banks of 16 bits repeating every 16 bytes (4 f32 words), per the SDK cs_readelf/man-page rule [SDK]. Measured, 1,024 elements, two source streams [Measured]:
    - f32 `@fmacs`: 1/cycle on both chips, except 2 cycles/element when the two sources start at addresses equal mod 16 B (same bank). Program 01 and Ch 6's SAXPY are this slow case.
    - f16 `@fmach`: WSE-3 ≈4/cycle (272) in every placement tested. WSE-2 ≈4/cycle (270) when the sources start 8 B apart mod 16 B (4 banks, "opposite halves"); ≈2/cycle (526) otherwise.
    - f32 `@fadds`: ≈2/cycle (WSE-3 527; WSE-2 526) when the sources start 8 B apart mod 16 B; WSE-2 drops to 1/cycle (1,038) at 0 or 4 B apart. Lab 3's `u[i]`/`u[i+2]` are 8 B apart, which is why its add is fast.
    - Teach it as ONE rule: "rates are layout-dependent; peak needs the two source streams in opposite halves of the 16-byte bank cycle; same-bank halves the rate." Retire as rates: "WSE-2 f16 = 2/cycle", "WSE-3 f16 = 3.2/cycle", "WSE-3 doubles f16". Retire as explanations: "memory traffic", "two reads and a write". Canonical home of the table: Ch 6 (bank section, anchor `#banks` — Ch 6 fixer creates/keeps it); other pages state the one-line rule and link there. App E issues 2 and 3 merge into one Measured issue.
31. **launch() (confirms 21).** `launch()` returns at once (re-measured: 0.00 s with a 63k-cycle RPC); the NEXT memcpy is what waits (5.06 s). Any text or code comment saying launch "waits until the device unblocks" is wrong (Ch 12, 13, 14, 15; programs/06-summa/pe_program.csl:123; programs/07-cg/pe_program.csl:115, 217).
32. **Per-user vs aggregate tok/s (confirms 25/27).** One user on 4 × CS-3, Llama-70B: ~2,000–4,000 tok/s (measured 2,314 sits inside). ~600k tok/s is the all-user aggregate. ~7,500 is an overstated headline-bandwidth bound — do not present it as the one-user ceiling anywhere (Ch 1, Ch 18 chooser, App A "Tokens/s").
33. **Clock.** No official WSE-3 clock. SDK benchmarks assume 850 MHz [SDK]. CS-4 press (The Next Platform, 2026-08-19) estimates ~1.4 GHz WSE-3 and ~2.8 GHz WSE-3T [Analyst/Unverified]. 34. **Hop costs (confirms 20/24).** Pure router hop 2.0 (WSE-3) / 1.0 (WSE-2) cycles; a PE that receives, adds and forwards ≈9.2 / 6.0 cycles per PE. The old "~7 cycles per PE" is an own-clock artefact; replace it everywhere. "One hop per cycle" is a hardware-paper figure; say the WSE-3 simulator models 2.
