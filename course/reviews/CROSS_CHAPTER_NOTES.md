# Cross-chapter corrections (coordinator)
Fixers: apply any item that touches your chapter.

1. **Microthread hang (ch03, ch05, ch12).** A rebuild of Lab 3 with input queues 2/3 fails on BOTH WSE-2 and WSE-3. sim.log shows a fatal error at about cycle 2,125: "trying to term ut_instr[3], but it's not ours". The host hangs. Two microthreads collide (2 and 3). Per topic-15, queue ID = microthread ID is the WSE-2 rule; on WSE-3 it is the default when no `.ut_id` is given. Do not claim "stalled forever at cycle 1,894" (not reproducible). Source: reviews/ch12-accuracy.md.
2. **Clock (ch01, ch02, ch03, ch15).** Hot Chips 2022 (Sean Lie) gives the WSE-2 clock as 1.1 GHz and 30 mW peak per core [Vendor]. No WSE-3 clock is published. The SDK benchmarks assume 850 MHz. Source: reviews/ch08-depth.md; deck in /home/user/cerebras-sdk/tmp/ch08depth/.
3. **CS-6 wording (ch08, ch15).** Wafer-scale SRAM plus 3D-stacked DRAM, not "3D wafer-scale SRAM".
4. **Lab 3 timing (ch12, ch15).** Slopes from 100 vs 200 steps give per-step costs of 100 / 130 / 250 cycles for n = 4 / 16 / 64, which fits 90 + 2.5·n. Also WSE-2 126 vs WSE-3 130, P=2 101, P=16 ~130.6. The 0-step baseline is noisy. Source: reviews/ch12-accuracy.md.
5. **Lab 2 timing (ch11, ch15).** Total cycles are host memcpy only: an empty reduce() gives identical totals. TSC-measured kernel times: 58 / 73 / 101 / 158 cycles for P = 2 / 4 / 8 / 16 (M=32, WSE-3), about M + 7P. Source: reviews/ch11-accuracy.md.
6. **Cerebras decode is not memory-bandwidth bound (ch01, ch08, ch15).** The bandwidth ceiling on 4 × CS-3 is far above the measured ~2,100–2,314 tok/s. The limit is pipeline latency and communication. Sources: reviews/ch01-depth.md, reviews/ch08-depth.md, reviews/ch15-depth.md.
7. **Removing unblock_cmd_stream (ch07, ch09, ch10).** In the simulator this does not hang forever: after ~4 s cs_python aborts with "received length (0 bytes) is not expected … could be a kernel stall". Source: reviews/ch10-pedagogy.md and reviews/ch10-accuracy.md.
8. **Halo columns (ch02, ch07, ch09).** 1 halo + 3 memcpy columns on the west (hence offset 4), 2 memcpy + 1 halo on the east, so the fabric is 7 columns wider and 2 rows taller. Source: reviews/ch07-depth.md.
9. **"At least" (ch07, ch09).** The user rectangle must be AT LEAST 7 columns narrower and 2 rows shorter than the fabric.
10. **Clock in sanity checks (ch03, ch04).** Replace "~1 GHz" with WSE-2 1.1 GHz [Vendor, HC22] and the SDK's 850 MHz. Ch02 now shows a per-PE budget at both clocks.
11. **Queue collision measured (ch12, ch15).** Fails on both chips: FATAL at cycle 2,123 (WSE-3) / 2,125 (WSE-2), "trying to term ut_instr[3], but it's not ours". Giving the receives `.ut_id` 4/5 fixes it on WSE-3. One shared completion task also hangs, on both chips (measured by the ch05 fixer).
12. **Task IDs (ch07, ch09, ch10–12).** Recommend user local task IDs 8–20. Control task IDs are [0,64). Data tasks on WSE-3 must bind to input queues. Do not bind your own task to 29.
13. **Memory budget (ch09, ch10, ch12).** The fixed overhead of a minimal program is ~4.6 KB on WSE-3 (3.2 KB code + 1 KB task table) and ~3.1 KB on WSE-2. About 44 KB is usable for user arrays on WSE-3, not ~46 KB. Source: reviews/ch03-depth.md.
14. **Per-PE speed (ch06, ch10–12).** f32 multiply-accumulate runs at ~1 element/cycle on both chips. f16 runs at ~3.2/cycle on WSE-3 and ~2.0 on WSE-2 (simulator). Source: reviews/ch03-depth.md.
15. **Blackhole numbers (ch01, ch15).** L1 1536 KiB (Wormhole 1464). NoC flits 64 B, packets ≤16 KiB (Wormhole 32 B / 8 KiB). NoC at 1.35 GHz. Up to 64 circular buffers. The p150 has 4 × 800 GbE ports (8 of 14 Ethernet tiles wired); the p100 has no Ethernet. Peaks follow cores × 4096 FLOP/cycle × clock. Source: ch13-fixlog.md and the ch13 page.
16. **DSD op rates (ch03, ch10–12, ch15).** In the simulator (memory to memory, f32): @fmacs/@fmuls 1 element/cycle on both chips; @fadds 1/cycle on WSE-2 and 2 on WSE-3; @fmovs 2 and 4; about 15 cycles to start each op. Lab 3's 2.5 cycles per cell = 0.5 (fadds) + 1 (fmacs) + 1 (fmacs). Stepping a DSD in a scalar loop with @increment_dsd_offset costs ~33–35 cycles per step. Source: reviews/ch06-depth.md.
17. **Timing noise (ch09, ch12, ch15).** Whole-run sim_stats.json cycles vary with host load (+1–4%, sometimes more), and loaded runs can abort with "could be a kernel stall". In-kernel `<time>` TSC numbers are stable. Do NOT claim "±2 cycles". Recommend TSC for kernel timing. The per-cycle instruction trace is available via SINGULARITYENV_SIMFABRIC_DEBUG=inst_trace. Source: reviews/ch10-depth.md.
18. **Program 01 cost split.** @fmacs ≈130 cycles (2/elem f32); each H2D ≈1,360; D2H ≈4,400 of 7,740. Totals: WSE-3 ≈ 3,640 + 64·N. Source: reviews/ch10-depth.md.
19. **Timing across PEs (ch11, ch12, ch15).** TSC values on different PEs are not comparable in the simulator: each counter starts when the launch reaches that PE. Time on one PE, or use a round trip.
20. **Hop cost (ch11, ch15).** A pure router hop measured as a round trip on one PE costs 2.0 cycles/hop on WSE-3 and 1.0 on WSE-2, plus 1 cycle per extra wavelet on WSE-3. A WSE-3 route accepts at most 1 input direction (compile error with 2). Source: ch04 page and ch04-fixlog.md.
21. **launch() (ch07, ch10, ch15).** Returns to Python even if the device never unblocks. The next memcpy fails with "kernel stall", and no sim_stats.json is written (measured by the ch09 fixer).

22. (from ch10 fixer) Program 01's 2 cycles/element for @fmacs is an x/y SRAM bank clash; padding one word between the arrays gives 1 element/cycle on both chips. This reconciles notes 14/16 (1/cycle) with note 18 (2/element). f16 fmach: 4/cycle WSE-3, 2/cycle WSE-2. Exact clash rule is undocumented and differs by chip at N=64 — say "pad and measure".
23. (from ch06 fixer) Operand-collision rule: @fmacs runs at half speed (2,064 vs 1,040 cycles for 1,024 elems, both chips) exactly when its two source streams start at addresses equal mod 4 words; adds show no penalty. Empirical. Per-op startup ≈17 cycles. Lab 3 per-cell 2.5 = 0.5 + 1 + 1 under the 90 + 2.5·n model (not 88).
24. (from ch11 fixer) Hop through a PE's compute (recv, @fadds, send) ≈ 9.2 cycles WSE-3 / 6.0 WSE-2, vs pure router hop 2.0 / 1.0. Use c ≈ 9 / 6 for chain-reduce models. Kernel time is 1–3% of whole-run cycles in Lab 2 (rest is host I/O). Store-and-forward vs streaming at P=8, M=32: 348 vs 101 cycles. <collectives_2d> adds ≈700 fixed cycles.
