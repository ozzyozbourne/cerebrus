# R4 audit: ch13 (Lab 3: a heat stencil, measured)

Page: `13-lab-stencil.html` (from `src/13-lab-stencil.html`, program `programs/04-heat-stencil`).

**Verdict: Mostly correct. Explanation quality: good.** The numbers hold up. I recompiled and re-ran program 04 on WSE-3 in a scratch copy (now deleted). The default run gave TSC 6,549 cycles for 50 steps and 11,666 total cycles. The 10-step and 100-step runs gave 1,329 and 13,049, a slope of 130.2. n = 15 gave 1,609 and 15,849, a slope of 158.2, which confirms the bank clash. Every table slope recomputes from its 10/100-step columns, and every widget formula reproduces the measured totals. The defects are small: stale framing, wrong chapter references, and a few bridges the learner has to build alone.

## Errors

**E1 (low): stale "capstone" framing.** In the lede: "The capstone program simulates heat flowing…". Lab 3 is now the 3rd of 5 labs. Lab 4 (GEMM) and Lab 5 (CG) follow it and both refer back to it. Fix: "The third lab simulates…" or "The first full application…".

**E2 (low): wrong chapter reference for 16-bit memcpy.** In #tsc: "each u16 arrives in the low half of a 32-bit word, Chapter 3". The Half-precision exercise hint has the same "(Chapter 3)" reference. Chapter 3 covers 16-bit memory addressing, not memcpy. The `MEMCPY_16BIT` behaviour, where the host array stays 32-bit and the upper half is stripped, is explained in Chapter 7 (`07-host-io.html`). Change both references to Chapter 7.

**E3 (low): the ELF cited for the inlining trap is an end PE, but the timed PE is interior.** The text says "each completion task grew from 176 to 212 bytes in the ELF (readelf -sW out/bin/out_3_0.elf)". In my build of the shipped program (P=8, n=16, WSE-3), `out_0_0.elf` and `out_3_0.elf` have 176-byte `t_se`/`t_rw`, and `out_1_0.elf` and `out_2_0.elf` have 200 bytes. cslc emits one ELF per distinct code variant, not one per PE, so `out_3_0` is an end-PE variant. The 130.2 → 167.0 timing was taken on PE 1, which is interior, so its tasks are 200 bytes before the change. Fix: say the figures are for an end-PE ELF, or quote the interior ELF's sizes. Also add one clause explaining that `bin/` holds one ELF per code variant.

**E4 (low): the timeline widget's edge case disagrees with the text's decomposition.** The widget adds a 1.6-cycle issue slot per op on top of 14.6 per completion task (`issueGap = 1.6`, `tlBuild`). Dropping to 2 transfers therefore removes 32.4 cycles, and the "end of rod" preset shows 97.7 against "measured 101.0 at P = 2". The prose says each transfer costs ≈14.6 *including* its issue and task (29.2 saved). Fix: fold the issue slot into the 14.6 (for example 13.0 task + 1.6 issue), or label the edge result "model 97.7, measured 101.0 (model double-counts issue)".

**E5 (low): the microthread limit is credited to WSE-3 only.** In Scaling up, 2-D and 3-D: "On WSE-3, eight concurrent async operations would need eight microthreads beyond memcpy's". The limit applies to both chips. On WSE-2 the microthread ID is fixed to the queue ID, so the constraint is at least as tight there. State the actual count: queues and microthreads 2–7 are free, so 6 concurrent ops at most (Chapter 5's table).

**E6 (low): the 7-point library's `.ut_id` serialisation is WSE-3 only.** The "How the SDK's stencils differ" table and text say "every send goes on one explicitly chosen microthread (`.ut_id = out_ut`)". `benchmark-libs/stencil_3d_7pts/wse2/pe.csl:26` says "output_ut_id is NOT used in WSE2". Add "(WSE-3 build)".

**E7 (low): a provenance tag is mislabelled.** CS-1 BiCGSTAB "0.86 PFLOP/s, roughly a third of peak" is tagged "Measured by Cerebras/NETL authors". By the course's own tag scheme, `measured` means this course's runs. Use Vendor, since it is a paper co-authored by Cerebras (arXiv:2010.03660).

**Cross-page (not this file):** `05-tasks.html` still says "Lab 3's measured ~88 fixed cycles per step (Chapter 13)". It should say ≈90 (90.2), and it could point to the 14.6-per-transfer split here.

## Explanation problems

**X1: almost none of the ~15 chapter references are links.** The page cites Chapters 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 11 and 12 throughout, but only three links exist, all to `06-dsds.html`. It also has no glossary links. Make every "Chapter N" a link. Link first uses to the Appendix A entries for ghost cell/halo, microthread, TSC, SpMV, pencil, weak/strong scaling and Dirichlet boundary.

**X2: the steady-state hint gives two settling-time estimates without reconciling them.** It says "√(2αt) cells in t steps" (which implies L²/(2α) ≈ 32,800 steps for L = 128) and also "L²/(π²α) steps to settle" (≈6,640). The second is the e-folding time of the slowest mode, not the settling time. At 6,640 steps the deviation is still ≈0.12; my numpy check matched the page's 0.48 / 0.16 / 0.07 / 0.017. Say "L²/(π²α) is the time for the error to fall by e; allow 3–5× that".

**X3: the "Use the model" readout implies a flop count it never states.** The whole-wafer figure uses 5 flops per cell, which is stated in Scaling up but not in the widget. Add "(5 flops/cell)" to the readout line.

**X4: the per-cell slope of "exactly" 2.5 is overstated on WSE-2.** "The per-cell slope is 2.5 on both" is fine on WSE-3, but the WSE-2 data give 2.50 for n = 4→16 and 2.53 for n = 16→64. Use "≈2.5", or add "(2.53 from 16→64)".

**X5: the 25-point "≈123.6 cycles per z-cell" has an unclear unit.** It is unclear whether this is per step, per PE, or whole-run. Say what the benchmark's run.py prints and over what it divides.

## Connecting gaps

**G1 (med, S): the formula on the page is not linked to the physics or to why α = 0.25.** The update rule appears with "α = 0.25", and stability is mentioned only inside the rod widget's readout ("α > 0.5: unstable"). Missing: one or two sentences saying that α = DΔt/Δx², that the explicit scheme is stable only for α ≤ 0.5 (von Neumann), and that 0.25 is chosen safely inside that limit and also makes the multiplies exact in f32, which is why the result is bit-identical. Two facts the page already states separately ("bit-identical at α = 0.25" and the widget regimes) would then explain each other.

**G2 (med, S): the formula u[i−1], u[i], u[i+1] is not mapped to the three DSDs.** The code uses `left = u[i]`, `mid = u[i+1]`, `right = u[i+2]` over i = 0..n−1, which is a one-index shift because of the ghost cell. The prose says only "three vector instructions". Add a three-row mapping: cell j = i+1 has left neighbour u[i], itself u[i+1] and right neighbour u[i+2]. Also spell out the `@fmacs` operand order (`dst = a + b·s`, from Chapter 6's table) for `@fmacs(lap, lap, mid, −2.0)`. Link Chapter 6's "Lab 3 stencil" preset in the DSD widget, which already draws exactly this.

**G3 (med, S): the arch-specific code paths are unexplained.** The `fabout()` helper and `@initialize_queue` use `@is_arch("wse3")` (WSE-3 binds the colour to the output queue, WSE-2 puts `fabric_color` in the DSD). The prose never says why. Chapter 12 teaches this, so add one sentence with a back-link: "same WSE-2/WSE-3 split as Lab 2's send".

**G4 (med, S): "≈14.6 cycles per transfer" has no mechanism behind it.** The page concludes "the cost is the CE running a task for each transfer", but no chapter says what a task activation costs or why: picker latency, task entry and exit, the inlined op_done body. Appendix B now quotes the 14.6 back. Add a sentence that this is the course's best measurement of "issue + task dispatch + task body" and that it grows with task size (the 176→212-byte inlining result is the evidence). Fix Chapter 5's stale ~88 at the same time.

**G5 (med, S): the SDK 7-point SpMV library is not linked forward to Lab 5.** The "How the SDK's stencils differ" section analyses the `stencil_3d_7pts` library, which Lab 5 (Chapter 15, CG) actually uses (11 mentions there). Add a forward pointer: "Lab 5 calls this library as its SpMV". The halo-cost lesson here explains Lab 5's SpMV cost.

**G6 (low, S): 1-D halo is not linked to Lab 4's 2-D communication.** Scaling up ends with 2-D tiles needing 8 colours. Lab 4 is next and is 2-D, but the page does not point to it. Add one line: "Lab 4 moves to a 2-D grid, broadcasting along rows and columns instead of exchanging halos, and gets the opposite result: compute dominates."

**G7 (low, S): the TSC is introduced as if new.** "The SDK's own stencil benchmarks time kernels with … TSC, and so does this program" reads as a first introduction, but Labs 1 and 2 already call `enable_tsc()`. Add "as in Labs 1 and 2". The new material here is pack() and the slope method.

**G8 (low, S): the "at most one step apart" claim is asserted rather than shown.** In "No race on the ghosts", a two-line argument would make the objective "explain why it cannot race or hang" concrete. To finish step s+1 a PE needs its neighbours' step-s+1 edge values, so each neighbour must have reached step s+1. A send completes once the wavelet leaves the output queue, so a PE can be at most one wavelet ahead per direction.

**G9 (low, S): "weak scaling" is used before it is defined.** "Weak scaling is flat" appears before strong scaling is defined by example. Define both in a sentence, or link to the glossary.
